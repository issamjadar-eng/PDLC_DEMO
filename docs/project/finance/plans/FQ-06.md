## Question

DSO and over-90 AR by GPO and region, inventory days by product line — where is cash trapped?

**Asked by**: cfo, controller · **cadence**: monthly

## Goal — the decision this answer serves

The monthly working-capital review: which purchasing channel needs a collections
escalation (and whether shipments should be held against a payment plan), which legacy
lines need an inventory run-down or obsolescence reserve, and what the working-capital
problem is worth in cash. The failure mode this question catches is a near-target
company DSO hiding one channel's dispute, and a healthy flagship hiding the legacy
inventory build.

## Approach

Operational definitions this answer commits to:

- **Latest** = the maximum `period` in the pinned `finance/internal-ar-inventory`
  snapshot; all point-in-time figures are at that period. History = every period in
  the pin.
- **Days-type ratios aggregate by implied daily flow, never by averaging**: DSO over a
  slice = Σ AR ÷ Σ (AR ÷ dso_days); inventory days = Σ inventory ÷ Σ (inventory ÷
  inventory_days). Over-90 share = Σ over-90 ÷ Σ AR.
- **Cuts**: DSO and over-90 share per channel (three GPOs + direct) and per region;
  inventory days per product line.
- **Thresholds** (all stand-ins): company DSO target `dso_target_days` (50); channel
  over-90 escalation `over90_share_threshold_pct` (15%); inventory target cover
  `inventory_target_days` (75).
- **Cash trapped** = over-90 AR (latest, all channels) + Σ per line max(0, inventory −
  (inventory ÷ days) × target days).
- **Trend** = monthly over-90 share per channel (four lines) and monthly inventory days
  per line (three largest lines by inventory value individually, the rest combined).
- **Subscription AR: NOT IN THE CUBE.** cloud-suite billing is published as an
  unavailable series; company DSO covers device lines only.
- Compare on unrounded values; round only at render.

## Assumptions & expectations

- **E-06.1** — Company DSO stays at or below target _(basis: treasury working-capital
  target carried forward; no board-adopted target on file [VERIFY])_.
- **E-06.2** — No channel's over-90 receivables exceed the escalation threshold _(basis:
  credit-policy escalation trigger; not confirmed against the credit policy on file
  [VERIFY])_.
- **E-06.3** — Inventory days per product line stay within target cover _(basis:
  operations planning target; no S&OP policy names a ceiling [VERIFY])_.
- **Rollup-survival rule**: the verdict headline carries company DSO vs target, the
  worst channel with its over-90 share and the count of channels breaching, the lines
  over target cover, and the trapped-cash total.
- The cube generator is seeded with one channel's over-90 climbing and legacy inventory
  days rising; its corpus assert proves those knobs — the answer must still compute
  them and name the channel / lines from the data.

## Assertions & limits

This answer asserts: DSO and over-90 exposure by channel and region at the latest
period, inventory days per line, and the cash held above target. It does **not**
assert: bad-debt provision amounts, net working capital (no payables), the cause of a
channel's slow payment (dispute vs terms — needs the contract), or consumable
pull-through (FQ-07).
