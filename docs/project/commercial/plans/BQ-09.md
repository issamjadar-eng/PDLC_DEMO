## Question

Are we winning displaced accounts where an incumbent has an open recall or integration disruption — and how long does the window stay open?

**Asked by**: board-chair, cco · **cadence**: quarterly

## Goal — the decision this answer serves

Whether the commercial team should run a deliberate displacement play when an
incumbent takes a recall — and how fast it must move before the window closes. The
answer joins our (fabricated) CRM opportunity log to REAL openFDA recall postings, so
the join mechanics are real even though the win/loss records are demo-seeded — stated
in the report, never blurred.

## Approach

Committed definitions:

- **Population**: decided opportunities (`won`/`lost`) whose `incumbent_vendor` is a
  competitor (excludes `PainEase` — our own installed base — and `none` greenfield).
  `no-decision` is excluded and counted separately.
- **Firm canonicalization**: `incumbent_vendor` values and openFDA `recalling_firm`
  variants both map through the versioned alias file (`entity-aliases.yml`,
  case-insensitive prefix match, first hit wins) so "BD" joins "CareFusion 303, Inc."
  and "ICU Medical, Inc" joins "ICU Medical Inc". Unmatched firms stay raw and visible.
- **In-window** = at least one recall by the (canonical) incumbent with
  `event_date_posted` in the `disruption_window_days` (180, from `commercial.yml`)
  ending at the opportunity's `close_date`. Recall rows with no posted date are
  excluded (stated). Recall *scope/severity* is not modeled — any FRN recall posting
  counts (limit stated below).
- **Window comparison**: win rate in-window vs out-of-window over decided incumbent
  opportunities.
- **Window decay**: win rate bucketed by months since the incumbent's most recent
  prior recall at close (0–3, 3–6, 6–12, >12 months / no prior recall) — the "how
  long does it stay open" evidence. Small-n buckets carry their n and are not
  headline material.
- **Verdict phrasing derives from the bucket shape.** The computation claims "decay"
  only when the bucket win rates actually decrease across the interior buckets;
  otherwise the verdict states the observed profile (e.g., highest-first-bucket with
  a flat interior, or no monotone pattern). A data-shape claim is never hardcoded
  prose that survives a snapshot whose data says otherwise.
- **Window sensitivity (committed)**: the in/out split is recomputed at the
  `sensitivity_window_days` (90, from `commercial.yml`) window and reported directly
  under the 180-day split table. The binary contrast at 180 days is partly a
  window-definition artifact (the dense recall calendar pushes most opportunities
  in-window), and the sensitivity line keeps the quotable table honest.
- **Density caveat (committed up front)**: large incumbents recall often (Baxter has
  27 FRN postings since 2021), so most incumbent opportunities are mechanically
  "in-window"; the out-of-window control group is small. The decay buckets, not the
  binary split, carry the interpretive weight.
- **History**: quarterly decided counts, split in-window vs out-of-window, won vs
  lost (4 lines, zero-filled).

## Data

Registered corpus dependencies:
- `commercial/internal-winloss` — demo-fabricated CRM log (seeded elevated win rate vs
  Baxter/ICU incumbents from 2025-07 as a disruption stand-in).
- `commercial/openfda-recalls-infusion` — REAL FDA recall postings, product code FRN,
  2021→present.

Data we have: opportunity outcomes + real recall posting dates. Data we do NOT have:
recall severity/scope per record (not modeled), integration-disruption events that are
not recalls (the question's "or integration disruption" arm has no dataset — stated as
a gap), and account-level evidence that the buyer *knew* of the recall.

## Assumptions & expectations

- No catalog expectations are declared for this question; the expectations table will
  be empty rather than invented.
- The join treats a recall posting as a proxy for incumbent disruption at the account.
  That proxy is untested: the fabricated CRM's disruption windows were seeded
  independently of the real recall calendar, so the measured lift is a demo of the
  method, not market evidence — the report must say this in the verdict block.

## Assertions & limits

This answer asserts: the in-window vs out-of-window win-rate split and its decay
profile under the committed 180-day window, over canonicalized firm joins. It does NOT
assert: causality (recall → win), any severity weighting, anything about
integration-disruption events (no dataset), or market-real effect sizes — the
opportunity rows are fabricated; only the recall dates are real.
