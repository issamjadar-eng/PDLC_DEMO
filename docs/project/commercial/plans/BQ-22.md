## Question

Of last year's field signals, how many landed as design inputs or upgrade-pipeline items — and how many died in a spreadsheet?

**Asked by**: vp-quality · **cadence**: quarterly

## Goal — the decision this answer serves

The closed-loop audit of post-market surveillance: does a field signal actually reach
the design organization, or does it stall in the register? The decision served:
whether the VP of Quality escalates the signal-review process (CAPA on the process
itself, SLA enforcement, register triage) or the loop is demonstrably closing. This is
the number an ISO 13485 / FDA QSR auditor asks for in the post-market → design-input
feedback clause.

## Approach

Operational definitions this answer commits to:

- **As-of anchor** = the latest date present in the pinned register (max of opened
  and closed dates) — no clocks; every window derives from the pin.
- **Trailing-12-month cohort** = signals with `opened_date` in the 365 days ending at
  the anchor. The **lifetime view** (all register signals) is published alongside —
  the question says "last year's", but process health needs both.
- **Disposition funnel buckets** (from the register's disposition vocabulary):
  - **Landed in design** = `design-input` or `requirement-change` — the signal
    changed what we build.
  - **Landed in the upgrade pipeline** = `upgrade-item` — the signal changed what we
    ship to the field.
  - **Monitoring** = an explicit, closed decision to watch — neither landed nor dead.
  - **Died in a spreadsheet** = `no-action` **plus** signals still `open` more than
    90 days after opening at the anchor (`sla_days` in `commercial.yml`). A signal
    nobody dispositioned inside the SLA is functionally dead even though the register
    calls it open — counting only `no-action` would flatter the process.
  - **Recent open** = open ≤ 90 days — in-process, counted in neither camp.
- **E-22.1 (SLA)** = share of the cohort dispositioned within 90 days of opening:
  within-SLA = `closed_date − opened_date` ≤ 90; breach = dispositioned late OR open
  > 90 days at the anchor; open ≤ 90 days = pending (excluded from the denominator,
  counted and stated). One late disposition fails the 100% expectation.
- **Time-to-disposition distribution** = days opened→closed for all dispositioned
  signals, bucketed (0–30 / 31–60 / 61–90 / 91–120 / 121–180 / >180) — the comparison
  form; the 90-day SLA line falls between buckets three and four by construction.
  The reported median is the standard statistical median — the mean of the middle
  pair when the count is even (a red-team pass caught an upper-middle-element
  implementation overstating it).
- **Loop-closed exemplars** are enumerated by ID with their disposition refs (SIG →
  DI-/REQ-/UPG-) — the auditor-facing proof that the loop CAN close, alongside the
  count of those that did not.

## Data

Registered corpus dependencies:
- `commercial/internal-signal-register` — signal disposition register
  (demo-fabricated)

Data we have: the register (signal, source, category, disposition, disposition ref,
closure date). Data we still need (stated gaps, never faked): verification that the
DI-/REQ-/UPG- disposition refs actually exist in the requirements / upgrade systems —
the register asserts the landing, and this analysis trusts the register; a cross-system
trace (register ↔ DHF requirements ↔ upgrade campaign) is future work. No
dead-signal *reason* field exists — why a signal got `no-action` is not recorded.

## Assumptions & expectations

- **E-22.1** — Every field signal reaches a disposition within the review SLA
  _(basis: stand-in for the QMS signal-review SLA [VERIFY against the post-market
  SOP] — unvalidated, challengeable)_. Evaluated per the committed SLA definition
  above; 90 days is `sla_days` in `commercial.yml`, not yet traced to the SOP.
- The register is assumed to be the complete population of recognized field signals —
  signals never entered cannot be audited here (a capture-completeness question for
  the post-market process, stated as a limit).
- `disposition_ref` values are assumed truthful pointers into the design/upgrade
  systems (see the stated trace gap).

## Assertions & limits

This answer asserts: the disposition funnel (trailing-12-month and lifetime), the
died-in-a-spreadsheet share under the committed definition, the SLA verdict for
E-22.1, the time-to-disposition distribution, and named exemplars where the loop
closed. It does **not** assert: that landed signals were correctly *implemented*
downstream (needs the cross-system trace), why dead signals died (no reason field),
whether any individual `no-action` was clinically wrong (that is a review judgment,
not a register fact), or capture completeness of the register itself.
