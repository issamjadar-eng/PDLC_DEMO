## Question

Do we have the capacity to execute the remaining waves on schedule — or do we add
headcount, contract labor, or slip?

**Asked by**: vp-field-service · **cadence**: monthly

## Goal — the decision this answer serves

The monthly hire / contract / slip call: whether field-service leadership adds
headcount or contract labor for the remaining campaign waves, converts on-site work to
remote, or re-baselines the close date with customer notification. The answer must show
the capacity gap per region in units leadership can act on (completions per week), and
must flag stalls as distinct from slow-but-moving regions.

## Approach

Operational definitions this answer commits to (refined per the adversarial
verification / red-team findings on edition 2026-07-22.3):

- **Completed** = status `completed` or `completed-after-retry` in the pinned campaign
  snapshot; **remaining** = every other status (`scheduled`, `failed-pending-retry`,
  `rolled-back`); **on-site-remaining** = remaining rows with method `onsite`;
  **attempted** excludes still-`scheduled` devices.
- **Derivation strings are self-sufficient**: the data.json derivation `method` strings
  name these status sets explicitly, so a pins-only re-deriver (the adversarial-verify
  protocol forbids reading this plan) reproduces the same population first try —
  the definitions must not live only in this plan (red-team finding F26-1,
  edition 2026-07-27).
- **Run-rate anchor** = the pinned campaign snapshot's **as-of date** (the date part of
  the snapshot id) — NOT the latest completed_date. Anchoring at the last completion
  silently drops zero-completion days and is optimistic in exactly the situations that
  matter (stalls). The report carries an **anchor-sensitivity row** showing current and
  required rates under the alternative latest-completion anchor, and states whether the
  verdict changes.
- **Current rate** = completions in the trailing 28-day window ending at the anchor ÷ 4.
- **Required rate** = remaining devices ÷ weeks from the anchor to the campaign close
  date declared in `commercial.yml`.
- **Uniform stall criterion, applied to every region identically**: zero completions in
  the trailing 28-day window ending at the anchor, with work remaining, = a **stalled
  region** — reported as a materialized Issue, never a Risk, regardless of what any
  alternative-window arithmetic says its "rate" is.
- **Thin-margin watch rule**: a region that is on track (current ≥ required) but with
  headroom below the configured guard (current < required × (1 + thin_margin_headroom_pct/100))
  is surfaced as a Watch item and named in the verdict — on-track-but-thin is a
  finding, not silence. The guard lives in `commercial.yml` params.
- **FSE-days sizing (duration basis)**: remaining on-site workload per region =
  on-site-remaining devices × mean completed on-site duration_min ÷ fse_day_minutes.
  This is labor-time-only — travel and overhead are not in the campaign data — so it is
  a stated **lower bound**, a bridge until the FSE roster dataset lands.
- **Remote-conversion sizing (connectivity join)**: the "convert on-site to remote"
  mitigation is sized by joining on-site-remaining devices to `internal-fleet`
  connectivity by device_serial. Only connected devices are convertible; the count is
  reported per region even (especially) when it is zero.

## Data

Registered corpus dependencies:
- `commercial/internal-upgrade-campaign`
- `commercial/internal-fleet` (connectivity join for remote-conversion sizing)

Data we have: per-device campaign telemetry (status, dates, durations, method, region)
and per-device fleet connectivity. Data we still need (stated gaps, never faked): the
FSE roster/utilization dataset — until it exists, capacity conclusions are run-rate
projections plus a duration-basis workload lower bound, and the answer must say so;
travel/overhead time per on-site visit — until it exists, FSE-days sizing is
labor-time-only.

## Assumptions & expectations

- **E-26.1** — Existing field-service capacity absorbs the remaining waves without
  surge or slip _(basis: implicit in the wave plan; never validated against an FSE
  roster — unvalidated, challengeable)_.
- The trailing-window run-rate is assumed representative of near-term capacity; this is
  weakest for regions with short history or recent stalls, and the answer must not
  present projections from stalled regions as meaningful forecasts.
- The as-of anchor treats the snapshot date as "today" for windowing — deterministic
  against the pin, no clocks. The direction of error vs the latest-completion anchor is
  disclosed in the sensitivity row rather than absorbed.

## Assertions & limits

This answer asserts: the per-region capacity gap (current vs required completions/week)
under a stated anchor with a stated sensitivity, which regions are stalled vs slow vs
on-track-but-thin (uniform criteria), the labor-time lower bound of the remaining
on-site workload in FSE-days, how much of the on-site backlog is remote-convertible
today (connectivity join), and whether E-26.1 holds. It does **not** assert: the cause
of a stall (scheduling vs technical — needs field input), true visit cost or total FSE
demand (travel/overhead and roster are stated gaps), or a recommendation between
hire / contract / slip — it equips that decision, it does not make it.
