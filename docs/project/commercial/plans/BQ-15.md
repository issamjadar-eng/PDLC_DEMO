## Question

Is the state-of-the-art analysis still current (EU MDR), and are our headline spec
differentiators still ahead?

**Asked by**: vp-product, regulatory-affairs · **cadence**: annual

## Goal — the decision this answer serves

Whether regulatory affairs commissions a **formal refresh of the EU MDR
state-of-the-art analysis** now or lets it ride to its scheduled review — and whether
product can keep leading with the two headline differentiators (±0.35% flow accuracy,
150-hour battery) or must soften them. The SOTA document itself is NOT parsed by this
computation: it is the config-referenced object under assessment; all evidence comes
from pinned corpus data.

## Approach

Operational definitions this answer commits to:

- **Differentiators** = the config constants (`differentiators`: flow_accuracy_pct
  0.35, battery_hours 150), matching the SOTA doc's headline claims. Each is compared
  against **every** competitor value of that attribute in the pinned features snapshot;
  numeric ranges score as the end most favorable to the competitor.
- **Spec-basis disclosure** (red-team RT-15.1): our 0.35 accuracy figure is the
  *laboratory-standard* spec ("±0.35% laboratory standard, ±2.5% market standard" per
  the SOTA doc), while competitor matrix cells are nominal/field specs — the headline
  multiple mixes bases. The answer therefore also computes the margin on our
  *volumetric-basis* spec (config `flow_accuracy_pct_volumetric`, ±0.5 per the
  competitive assessment) and states both: the "ahead" verdict must survive either
  basis, and the margin-erosion refresh trigger fires if EITHER basis stops being
  ahead.
- **Still ahead** = our value strictly better than the best competitor value
  (accuracy lower, battery higher). The comparison states **verified-cell coverage**
  honestly: how many competitor cells are `verified: yes` vs `verify`, and which
  products have no value at all (deliberately absent, per the dataset README).
- **SOTA currency anchor** = config `sota_doc_date` (2026-04-12, the date the SOTA
  analysis was adopted into the project docs — the doc carries no separate authoring
  date). **Currency signal** = FRN clearances in the pinned 510(k) snapshot with
  `decision_date` after that anchor, plus trailing-12-month clearance activity (ending
  at the snapshot's newest decision date) as context. FDA publication lag is stated:
  the snapshot's own coverage horizon is its newest decision date, not today.
- **Refresh verdict** (deterministic rule): a formal refresh is *warranted now* if any
  of — (a) a differentiator margin is eroded or eliminated by a pinned competitor
  value; (b) any predictive_monitoring row in the features snapshot reads `yes`;
  (c) the SOTA doc is older than `review_cadence_months` (12) at the 510(k) snapshot's
  acquisition date. Otherwise the verdict is *ride to scheduled review*, with the next
  review date computed from anchor + cadence.

## Data

Registered corpus dependencies:
- `commercial/external-competitor-features`
- `commercial/openfda-510k-infusion`

Data we have: curated competitor spec values with verification flags; public FRN
clearance records. Data we still need (stated gaps, never faked): accuracy and battery
specs for several competitor products are absent from the matrix (the computation
derives the exact absence lists from the pin and states them — the comparison covers
only vendors with documented values); non-FRN product codes (a SOTA-relevant clearance
outside FRN is invisible here).

## Assumptions & expectations

- The 12-month review cadence is a config stand-in for the QMS's PMS/SOTA review
  cadence, not a documented EU MDR obligation of this project — challengeable.
- `sota_doc_date` uses the doc's adoption date as its currency anchor; if the
  underlying analysis was authored earlier, currency is overstated — noted, not hidden.
- No catalog expectations are declared for this question.

## Assertions & limits

This answer asserts: whether each headline differentiator is still ahead of every
documented competitor value (with verified-cell coverage stated, and the accuracy
margin stated on both the lab and volumetric spec bases), the clearance
activity observed since the SOTA anchor date, and the deterministic refresh verdict
under the committed rule. It does **not** assert: EU MDR compliance of the SOTA
analysis (a regulatory judgment), superiority against undocumented competitor specs,
clinical meaning of the spec margins, or anything about the SOTA doc's internal
content — the doc is referenced, not parsed.
