## Question

How much plan revenue sits behind FDA decisions we haven't received — by regulatory dependency, with slip triggers?

**Asked by**: audit-committee, cfo · **cadence**: annual

## Goal — the decision this answer serves

The audit committee's read on how much of the long-range plan is exposed to regulatory
events that have not happened, and what a realistic slip in those events does to each
plan year. It equips risk-appetite and disclosure discussions, and the trigger
question "at what exposure level must the plan carry a formal contingency."

## Approach

Operational definitions this answer commits to:

- **Plan years** = FY2026 (sum of its four quarterly rows) through FY2030, from the
  pinned plan of record.
- **Dependent revenue** = plan rows whose `regulatory_dependency` is in
  `dependent_categories` (`pccp-enabled`, `new-submission` — per `commercial.yml`):
  revenue behind an FDA decision **not yet received**. `letter-to-file` is
  deliberately NOT dependent — it needs internal documentation, not an FDA decision;
  `cleared` needs nothing. The full four-way split is still reported.
- **Exposure %** = dependent revenue ÷ total plan revenue, per plan year. Guardrail
  `exposure_threshold_pct` (40) per E-05.1, evaluated **every** plan year.
- **Slip scenarios (derived, method stated)** for each of `slip_scenarios_months`
  (6, 12): dependent revenue is treated as earned uniformly within its plan year; a
  slip of M months moves M/12 of each year's dependent revenue into the following
  year. Revenue pushed past FY2030 leaves the FY2026–FY2030 window and is reported as
  the plan-window shortfall — not silently recovered. Non-dependent revenue is
  untouched. The scenario slips **all** dependent revenue uniformly; per-program slip
  granularity does not exist in the plan data (one dependency tag per row, no
  submission-event linkage) — stated.
- **Context stat (real public data)**: the median FDA review time (date_received →
  decision_date) across infusion-pump (product code FRN) 510(k) clearances in the
  pinned openFDA snapshot — the public base-rate for how long one review cycle runs.
  It contextualizes the slip magnitudes; it does NOT measure PCCP or De Novo/PMA
  timelines, and our own demo K-numbers are fabricated, so no our-vs-market claim is
  made.
- Fiscal-year labels map to ISO dates as YYYY-01-01 for timeseries x-axes.
- **History** = exposure-over-time needs successive plan versions; only plan version
  LRP-2026.1 is snapshotted, so the history series is marked unavailable rather than
  faked.

## Data

Registered corpus dependencies:
- `commercial/internal-revenue-plan` — plan of record with per-row
  regulatory_dependency (demo-fabricated; stand-in for the FP&A long-range plan).
- `commercial/openfda-510k-infusion` — real FRN 510(k) clearance records since 2021
  (openFDA, public domain).

Data we have: the full dependency-tagged plan and real review-interval base rates.
Data we still need (stated gaps, never faked): a submission-event register linking
plan rows to specific planned submissions with target dates (to slip per event rather
than uniformly); PCCP-specific review-time evidence (FRN 510(k) intervals are the
nearest public proxy and are labeled as context only); prior plan versions (exposure
trend).

## Assumptions & expectations

- **E-05.1** — Regulatory-dependent revenue stays below the exposure threshold in
  every plan year _(basis: stand-in risk-appetite threshold; the plan of record sets
  none — unvalidated, challengeable)_.
- The uniform-slip method is a stated simplification; its direction of error is
  neutral per year but the past-FY2030 truncation makes the 12-month scenario's
  window shortfall a hard number, not a guess.

## Assertions & limits

This answer asserts: plan revenue by regulatory_dependency per plan year, the
exposure % per year vs the threshold, the arithmetic effect of uniform 6- and
12-month slips (per year and on the plan-window total), and the public median FRN
review interval as context. It does **not** assert: the probability of any FDA
decision or its timing, PCCP-pathway review duration, which specific programs slip,
or a revised plan. Plan data is demo-fabricated (directional narrative only); the
review-interval stat is real public data.
