## Question

How concentrated are we — PCA franchise, top-3 GPOs, single systems — and what does losing one anchor GPO do to the 5-year plan?

**Asked by**: lead-independent-director · **cadence**: annual

## Goal — the decision this answer serves

The board's annual concentration-risk read: whether revenue is dangerously dependent
on one GPO relationship, a handful of accounts, or a single franchise — and what a
plausible worst-case (losing the anchor GPO) does to the plan trajectory. It equips
diversification and contract-renewal posture; it does not set contract strategy.

## Approach

Operational definitions this answer commits to:

- **Basis year** = `fy` in `commercial.yml` (FY2025 — the last complete fiscal year in
  the pinned account data). Concentration is computed on that year; the FY2024 →
  FY2026H1 history is charted for trend.
- **Revenue basis** = the account-level **direct book (hardware + subscription)** —
  the only revenue that is account-attributed in the pinned dataset. Consumables and
  service flow through distributors and are NOT in these shares; concentration on the
  total P&L could differ. Stated on every share.
- **Basis sensitivity (committed disclosure)**: the E-02.1 guardrail is worded against
  *annual revenue*, but the test denominator is the direct book — so the verdict is
  basis-dependent and the answer must say so, prominently, in the verdict line, the
  expectations table, and a dedicated report section. The disclosure computes, from
  the internal-financials ledger for the basis calendar year (FY = calendar year by
  dataset construction): (a) the direct book's coverage of total revenue, and (b) the
  anchor GPO's **floor share of total revenue** — a lower bound that assumes the
  anchor takes none of the distributor-routed consumables/service book, whose account
  attribution does not exist in the corpus. With the current pins the anchor breaches
  on the direct book while the floor-of-total does not breach; neither basis is
  silently preferred — both are reported until the denominator gap closes.
- **GPO concentration** = share of basis-year direct-book revenue by `gpo`
  (`independent` reported but never counted as a "GPO"). The **anchor GPO** = the
  largest by basis-year revenue. Guardrail: `concentration_threshold_pct` (30) per
  E-02.1.
- **Account concentration** = top-3 accounts' share of basis-year direct-book revenue
  (accounts are health systems; multi-site systems roll up under one account_id by
  construction of the dataset).
- **PCA-franchise share** = revenue share of `pca_franchise_lines` (PP3500 + PP3000 +
  cloud-suite, per `commercial.yml`): Cloud Suite subscriptions ride on the PCA
  installed base, so counting the franchise without them would understate dependence.
  The pumps-only figure is also reported.
- **Anchor-GPO loss scenario (derived, method stated)**: subtract the anchor GPO's
  basis-year (FY2025) direct-book revenue, held **constant**, from each plan year
  FY2026–FY2030 of the revenue plan of record (FY2026 = sum of its quarterly rows).
  Deliberately simple and stated as such: (a) the dent is NOT grown with the plan —
  if the anchor's book would have grown with the plan, the true dent is larger, so
  the scenario is a **floor**; (b) plan totals are all-revenue while the dent is
  direct-book only — also conservative; (c) no second-order effects (win-backs,
  competitor conversion) are modeled in either direction.

## Data

Registered corpus dependencies:
- `commercial/internal-sales-accounts` — account-level revenue + installed units by
  FY, with GPO affiliation (demo-fabricated; stand-in for the CRM export).
- `commercial/internal-revenue-plan` — the plan of record FY2026–FY2030 (demo-
  fabricated; stand-in for the FP&A long-range plan).
- `commercial/internal-financials` — quarterly revenue ledger (demo-fabricated);
  pinned solely for the basis-sensitivity disclosure (total-revenue denominator).

Data we have: everything needed for GPO/account/franchise shares on the direct book
and for the loss-scenario arithmetic. Data we still need (stated gaps, never faked):
account-attributed consumables/service revenue (total-P&L concentration); GPO contract
terms and renewal dates (which would turn a share into an actual at-risk timeline);
and any churn/win-back behavior data to make the loss scenario dynamic.

## Assumptions & expectations

- **E-02.1** — No single GPO carries more than 30% of annual revenue _(basis: generic
  concentration guardrail; no board-adopted limit — unvalidated, challengeable)_.
  Evaluated on the **direct-book** denominator (the only attributed one); the verdict
  is therefore basis-sensitive and the answer states the alternative floor-of-total
  figure alongside it — a not-met on the direct book is not proof the guardrail as
  worded (annual revenue) is breached.
- The loss scenario is a derived arithmetic scenario, not a forecast; its method and
  conservatisms are stated inline and in the series derivation.

## Assertions & limits

This answer asserts: FY2025 direct-book concentration by GPO, top-3 accounts, and PCA
franchise; the concentration trend across the three fiscal years on file; and the
arithmetic dent an anchor-GPO loss puts in the FY2026–FY2030 plan trajectory under the
stated constant-dent method; plus the basis-sensitivity disclosure (direct-book
coverage of total revenue and the anchor's floor share of total revenue). It does
**not** assert: total-P&L concentration (the floor-of-total figure is a lower bound,
not an attribution), the probability of losing the anchor GPO, contract-level
exposure or timing, or any behavioral response to a loss. Demo-fabricated data —
directional narrative only.
