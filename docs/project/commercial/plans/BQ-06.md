## Question

Is the concept pipeline converting, and how does our clearance cycle time compare with
our own history and with BD/Baxter?

**Asked by**: independent-director-rd · **cadence**: annual

## Goal — the decision this answer serves

The board's annual R&D-productivity read: whether to fund the concept pipeline as-is,
concentrate it, or look outside (licensing/M&A). This answer supplies the one
competitor-speed metric that is computable rather than guessed — public FDA review
intervals — as the external baseline for that discussion.

## Approach

Operational definitions this answer commits to:

- **Review interval** = decision_date − date_received from public FDA 510(k) records in
  the pinned snapshot; product code FRN, decisions since 2021.
- **Frequent filer** = applicant with ≥ 2 clearances in the window; per-applicant and
  overall medians (median, not mean — the distribution is skewed).
- **Historical view** = median interval per decision year.
- Applicant names are used as published by FDA; no entity normalization is applied here
  (unlike the MAUDE analysis) — same-company name variants may appear as separate rows.

## Data

Registered corpus dependencies:
- `commercial/openfda-510k-infusion`

Data we have: public clearance records with received/decision dates. Data we still need
(stated gaps, never faked): OUR OWN received→decision history — the program's K-numbers
are demo-fabricated, so the comparison's left side needs the internal regulatory log as
a corpus dataset; the internal concept register — concept→development conversion cannot
be measured without it.

## Assumptions & expectations

- FDA review interval is assumed a fair proxy for regulatory-phase speed across
  applicants; it says nothing about development speed before submission.
- The FRN-only scope is assumed adequate for the infusion-pump peer set; adjacent SaMD
  codes are out of scope here.

## Assertions & limits

This answer asserts: the public review-interval distribution (overall, per frequent
filer, per year) for FRN clearances since 2021. It does **not** assert: our own cycle
time (stated gap), concept-pipeline conversion (stated gap), develop-to-market speed,
or any claim about why an applicant's interval is fast or slow.
