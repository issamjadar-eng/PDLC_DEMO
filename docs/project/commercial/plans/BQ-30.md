## Question

What's our fully-loaded cost per update, remote vs on-site — and the business case for a remote-first campaign at the top non-connected accounts?

**Asked by**: vp-field-service, cfo · **cadence**: monthly

## Goal — the decision this answer serves

The remote-first investment call: whether field service funds connectivity adapters at
the biggest non-connected accounts to convert future update campaigns from on-site
visits to remote sessions — or keeps paying for truck rolls. The answer must cost both
methods from what the campaign data actually carries, bound what it cannot carry, and
never present a labor-only figure as "fully loaded".

## Approach

Operational definitions this answer commits to (worked from what the campaign rows
actually carry: status, method, completed_date, duration_min, tickets — there are NO
per-attempt timestamps, NO retry counts, and NO travel/overhead fields):

- **Completed update** = status `completed` or `completed-after-retry`. Method is taken
  from the campaign row; the fleet connectivity join (by device_serial) verifies the
  method↔connectivity alignment and is reported.
- **Remote cost per completed update** = `remote_session_cost_usd` from
  `commercial.yml`, one session per completed update. Retried updates consumed more
  than one session, but the data carries no attempt counts — stated limitation; the
  remote figure is therefore a floor.
- **On-site cost per completed update — bounded, not point-stated.** The campaign data
  has hands-on duration but no travel/overhead, so on-site cost is published as a
  range: **labor-time floor** = mean completed on-site `duration_min` ÷
  `fse_day_minutes` × `fse_day_rate_usd`; **full-day ceiling** = one FSE day
  (`fse_day_rate_usd`) per update, the conventional field-service costing when a visit
  consumes a dispatch. True fully-loaded cost sits between them and cannot be resolved
  without FSE travel/roster data — published as an `unavailable` series.
- **E-30.1 verdict rule (deterministic on the bounds)**: remote ≤ 25% of on-site is
  `met` if it holds even at the on-site floor, `not-met` if it fails even at the
  ceiling, else `at-risk` (the answer depends on the unmeasured travel component).
- **Remote-first business case** = for the top `top_n_accounts` sites ranked by
  non-connected PP3500 count (fleet registry): adapter capex = `adapter_unit_cost_usd`
  × non-connected devices, compared against per-campaign on-site cost under both
  bounds. Payback is expressed in update campaigns: adapter cost ÷ (on-site cost per
  update − remote cost per update), at each bound. No campaign-frequency assumption is
  made — payback is stated per campaign, not per year. The within-campaign breakeven
  (adapter + remote session cost per update) is stated as its computed position within
  the floor–ceiling bound — never as a directional adverb like "near the top of the
  bound" (red-team finding F30-1, edition 2026-07-27).

## Data

Registered corpus dependencies:
- `commercial/internal-upgrade-campaign` (per-device: method, status, completed_date, duration_min)
- `commercial/internal-fleet` (per-device: model, site, region, connected)

Data we have: completed-update durations by method; device connectivity; non-connected
device counts per site. Data we still need (stated gaps, never faked): travel and
overhead per on-site visit and FSE roster/day structure (blocks a true fully-loaded
on-site cost — hence the bounds); per-attempt session counts (remote cost is a floor);
finance-validated rates (all three config rates are demo stand-ins).

## Assumptions & expectations

- **E-30.1** — A remote update costs a fraction of an on-site visit _(basis:
  business-case stand-in; not yet validated by finance — unvalidated, challengeable)_.
  Evaluated with the deterministic bound rule above.
- Config rates (`fse_day_rate_usd`, `remote_session_cost_usd`,
  `adapter_unit_cost_usd`, `fse_day_minutes`) are declared plan constants cited as
  `[config: commercial.yml]`, not measured costs.

## Assertions & limits

This answer asserts: per-update cost by method under stated bounds, the E-30.1 verdict
under the bound rule, and adapter-vs-onsite payback per campaign at the top
non-connected accounts. It does **not** assert: a single fully-loaded on-site number
(travel data does not exist), annualized savings (campaign frequency is not committed
anywhere), adapter installation labor or site willingness (not in any dataset), or a
funding decision — it prices the trade, it does not approve it.
