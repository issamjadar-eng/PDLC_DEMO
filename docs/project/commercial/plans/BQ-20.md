## Question

Any field signal — complaints, near-misses, alarm data — around opioid over-delivery or PCA-by-proxy?

**Asked by**: cmo · **cadence**: monthly

## Goal — the decision this answer serves

The CMO's franchise-killer watch. For a PCA pump, opioid over-delivery and PCA-by-proxy
are the failure modes that end the franchise: **single events, not trends, are the
signal**. This answer exists so the CMO sees every watch-category event the month it
appears — with its MDR posture and its path into the corrective pipeline — and never
learns of one from FDA first. The decision served: escalate (CMO review, risk-file
update, field action) or continue watching.

## Approach

Operational definitions this answer commits to:

- **Watch categories** are declared in `commercial.yml` (`watch_categories:
  over-delivery, pca-by-proxy-suspected`) — the analysis enumerates records against
  that declared list; it does not discover categories.
- **Event-level reporting, not trend gating.** Watch-category complaint records are
  listed individually (ID, date, region, model/firmware, status, MDR flag). No rate
  or trend threshold is applied to them: under a zero-tolerance expectation, one
  event is the headline.
- **"Confirmed" for E-20.1** = any watch-category complaint record not dismissed as
  unfounded. The complaint log carries no dismissed/unfounded status, so every
  over-delivery record counts against zero tolerance — including records still
  `under-investigation` (a zero-tolerance watch escalates on suspicion; it does not
  wait for investigation closure).
- **Pipeline linkage**: signal-register entries in the watch categories are joined by
  category; their dispositions (e.g. `upgrade-item` → UPG refs) show whether the
  field-signal → corrective-pipeline loop is closing. The regulatory docket is a
  **registered, pinned corpus dependency** of this answer: open watch-category MDR
  item(s) are derived from the pinned docket rows and cited to the docket snapshot —
  never asserted as literals. The join remains category-level (no shared key exists
  between the complaint log and the docket) and is labeled as such wherever it
  appears.
- **Class-wide context** = real openFDA MAUDE monthly event counts for product code
  MEA (PCA pumps), aggregated from daily buckets to months. COUNTS ONLY — MAUDE has
  no denominator (the A-001 pattern), so **no rate is computed or published**. The
  trailing **three months** are EXCLUDED from the charted series: MAUDE reporting lag
  makes them artificially low and charting them would fake a decline. Three months is
  the lower bound of the dataset README's "~3–6 months" lag warning (deepened from the
  two-month sibling convention after a red-team finding); the report carries the
  caveat that the lag can run longer than the trim.
- **Fabricated vs real, never blended**: our internal events (demo-fabricated) and
  the class-wide MAUDE series (real public data) live in separate series and separate
  charts, and the verdict names which is which.

## Data

Registered corpus dependencies:
- `commercial/internal-complaints` — complaint log incl. the rare high-severity watch
  categories (demo-fabricated)
- `commercial/internal-signal-register` — signal dispositions incl. over-delivery
  signals (demo-fabricated)
- `commercial/openfda-maude-pca-monthly` — real MAUDE counts, product code MEA
- `commercial/internal-regulatory-docket` — MDR/field-action docket, source of the
  derived open watch-category MDR linkage (demo-fabricated)

Data we have: the complaint log, the signal register, the real PCA-class event-count
history. Data we still need (stated gaps, never faked): event-level alarm telemetry —
the question asks about "alarm data", but only signals *sourced from* alarm analytics
exist in the register; raw alarm data is not yet a corpus dataset. An installed-base
denominator for the MAUDE series (A-001) is deliberately unquantified — it blocks
rates, not counts.

## Assumptions & expectations

- **E-20.1** — Zero confirmed over-delivery events in the field _(basis:
  franchise-killer tolerance — any occurrence escalates to CMO; threshold not yet
  ratified in the risk file [VERIFY] — unvalidated, challengeable)_. Evaluated against
  the committed "confirmed" definition above.
- The three-month MAUDE lag exclusion follows the lower bound of the dataset README's
  "~3–6 months" lag warning — a convention, not a measured lag model; the lag can run
  longer than the trim, and the report states this.
- No assumption is made that internal complaint counts and MAUDE class counts are
  comparable populations — they are never compared numerically.

## Assertions & limits

This answer asserts: the enumerated watch-category events on our internal log, their
MDR/investigation posture, whether the signal→pipeline loop is closing for them, and
the real class-wide PCA event-count trend as context. It does **not** assert: any
event *rate* (internal or MAUDE — no denominator), any numeric comparison of our
events to the class (different populations and reporting propensities — and in this
demo, fabricated vs real data), the root cause of any event (the investigation owns
that), or that zero listed events would mean zero field occurrences (complaint capture
is not exhaustive).
