## Question

How does our adverse-event profile compare to competitors — and what denominator did you
use, because MAUDE doesn't have one?

**Asked by**: ceo, vp-quality · **cadence**: quarterly

## Goal — the decision this answer serves

Whether any comparative-safety statement can be made (to the board, or in sales
material) on public adverse-event data — and to make the epistemic limits of that data
impossible to overlook. The honest deliverable is counts with caveats and a mechanically
blocked rate chart until the denominator assumption is quantified.

## Approach

Operational definitions this answer commits to:

- **Counts, never rates**: MAUDE carries no denominator; a per-manufacturer RATE
  requires the installed-base assumption A-001, and until A-001 carries a reviewed
  model the rate chart is blocked by construction, not by convention.
- **Entity normalization** via the versioned alias map (entity-aliases.yml) —
  manufacturer name variants fold to canonical companies; unmatched names stay raw and
  visible.
- **Historical view** = class-wide monthly counts since 2023, with the trailing two
  months EXCLUDED — MAUDE reporting lag makes them artificially low and charting them
  would fake a decline.

## Data

Registered corpus dependencies:
- `commercial/openfda-maude-infusion-mfr`
- `commercial/openfda-maude-infusion-monthly`

Data we have: real public MAUDE counts (by manufacturer; by received date). Data we
still need (stated gaps, never faked): per-manufacturer installed bases (A-001 — not
yet quantified); any model of cross-manufacturer reporting propensity.

## Assumptions & expectations

- **A-001** — installed-base denominators: recorded, active, deliberately NOT yet
  quantified; its status gates the rate analysis.
- Reporting propensity differs across manufacturers — an unmodeled bias that persists
  even with a denominator; every comparative statement must carry it.

## Assertions & limits

This answer asserts: entity-normalized event counts and the class-wide monthly history
(lag-trimmed). It does **not** assert: rates, relative safety, or any claim usable in
promotion — a "most reliable in class" claim is unsupported by this data alone.
