## Question

Stacked against clearances, KOL data, and attach actuals — which roadmap bet do we
kill, and which do we pull forward a year?

**Asked by**: vp-product · **cadence**: annual

## Goal — the decision this answer serves

The annual roadmap-portfolio review: a single composite view of the F1–F9 bets across
the three evidence axes the catalog names — competitive pressure (parity gaps +
clearance activity), documented KOL sentiment (with its meta-gap), and demand actuals
(Cloud Suite attach) — surfacing ONE kill candidate and ONE pull-forward candidate.
**Decision support, not the decision**: the composite equips the council's judgment;
it does not replace it.

## Approach — the scoring method, fully stated

Per feature F1–F9, three components, each in [0, 1]:

1. **Competitive pressure** (derived from the features matrix + 510(k) snapshot):
   - `1.0` — a lane-mapped attribute (config `attribute_lane_map`, as in BQ-14) is
     *behind* with at least one competitor shipping today (`yes` row) while we do not
     ship (no `yes` row for us);
   - `0.5` — otherwise, if we are behind-but-`planned` on a lane-mapped attribute, OR
     any clearance in the trailing 12 months of the 510(k) snapshot (ending at its
     newest decision date) matches the lane's `lane_watch_keywords` on the public
     device name;
   - `0.0` — otherwise. A lane-mapped attribute with zero rows for every vendor
     contributes nothing (no evidence of pressure ≠ no pressure; stated).
2. **KOL sentiment** (assumption-class — the register is a simulated panel with zero
   real collected evidence, per the BQ-16 meta-gap): net sentiment
   (support − concern) ÷ distinct voices, rescaled from [−1, 1] to [0, 1].
3. **Demand actual** (measured): Cloud Suite attach rate = active subscribed connected
   sites ÷ all connected sites (subscriptions + fleet pins), applied to features
   delivered through the cloud/SaMD stack (config `cloud_features`); non-cloud features
   (F7 hardware, F9 international program) score 0 on this axis **by construction** —
   no demand dataset exists for them, and the composite must state that limit rather
   than read 0 as "no demand".

**Composite** = unweighted mean of the three components (weights are a config choice;
equal weighting is the committed default). **Eligibility** for verdicts = config
`eligible` (F4–F9): the Y1 features F1–F3 are already in-flight in 2026 and can be
neither killed nor pulled forward by this review; they are shown for context only.

- **Pull-forward candidate** = highest composite among eligible features; tie broken
  by the *earliest scheduled year* (cheapest pull).
- **Kill candidate** = lowest composite among eligible features; tie broken by the
  *fewest distinct KOL voices* (weakest evidence base). Ties and tiebreaks are
  reported, never silent.
- **Robustness checks** (committed, red-team RT-17.1 follow-ups — computed and
  reported every edition):
  1. *Tie-vector invariance*: if the features in the top (or bottom) tie share an
     identical (pressure, sentiment, demand) vector, the tie survives ANY axis
     weighting — the pick is then 100% tie-break-decided, and the answer says so.
  2. *Sentiment-axis drop*: the composite is recomputed without the simulated
     sentiment axis (mean of pressure + demand); whether both picks survive is
     reported — it tells the council whether the assumption-class axis is
     load-bearing for the candidates.
  3. *Weight-sensitivity scan*: the smallest pressure-to-sentiment weight transfer
     (0.25-pp steps, up to 10 pp) that flips the pull-forward pick is reported with
     the feature it flips to — small flip distances are stated, not buried in a
     generic "weights could reorder" caveat.
- **Sentiment-direction limitation** (red-team RT-17.1, HIGH): the register's 3-value
  vocabulary cannot carry the *direction* of a concern. Where the kill candidate's
  evidence base is a single voice, the answer must state that voice's actual position
  next to the kill line — concretely, the sole F9 voice (Kuitunen) argues F9 is "too
  late and too thin" (start EU evidence EARLIER, Y1–Y2), i.e. a voice FOR earlier
  investment that the encoding arithmetically converts into kill support. A kill
  recommendation must carry this prominently (verdict-level, not footnote).

## Assumptions & expectations

- The KOL sentiment axis inherits the register's epistemic status: simulated panel,
  zero real collected evidence — the composite's sentiment third is assumption-class
  and every verdict carries that caveat.
- Equal weighting of the three axes is a committed modeling choice, not derived from
  the plan of record; a different weighting can reorder candidates — stated, with the
  computed flip distance (the weight-sensitivity scan) rather than a generic caveat.
  The two picks themselves are expected to be tie-break-decided and
  weighting-invariant (identical tie vectors) — verified per edition, not assumed.
- Cloud attach is a proxy for demand for not-yet-shipped cloud features — it measures
  appetite for the current Cloud Suite, not for F4/F6/F8 specifically.
- No catalog expectations are declared for this question.

## Assertions & limits

This answer asserts: the per-feature component scores and composite under the committed
method, the resulting kill and pull-forward candidates with ties and tiebreaks shown,
the three robustness checks (tie-vector invariance, sentiment-axis drop,
weight-sensitivity flip distance), the direction of the kill candidate's evidence
where it rests on a single voice, and the evidence class of every input. It does
**not** assert: the decision itself (council judgment), business-case economics (no
revenue axis), demand for non-cloud features (no data), that the sentiment encoding
carries a voice's direction (it cannot — 3-value vocabulary), or anything grounded in
real KOL opinion (none exists yet). A kill/pull-forward verdict from this composite is
a derived, assumption-laden ranking — framed as decision support.
