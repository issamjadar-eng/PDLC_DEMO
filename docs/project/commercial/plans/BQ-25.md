## Question

Are customers struggling with this upgrade — tickets per 100 upgraded pumps, rollbacks —
and what does an upgrade cost the customer?

**Asked by**: customer-success, cfo · **cadence**: weekly

## Goal — the decision this answer serves

The customer-effort early warning during a campaign: rising tickets or rollback
clusters are the signal to pause, fix documentation/training, or escalate a defect
before it becomes a field-action conversation — plus the customer-side cost picture the
Cloud Suite attach case needs.

## Approach

Operational definitions this answer commits to:

- **Tickets per 100** = tickets summed over ATTEMPTED devices ÷ attempted devices × 100
  — numerator and denominator on the SAME basis (adopted after adversarial verification
  showed a mixed basis flipped which region looked worst).
- **Rollback sites** = distinct sites with a `rolled-back` device.
- **Customer cost** = completed on-site updates × the A-002 model (hours-per-device ×
  benchmark labor rate) — an ASSUMED estimate, low confidence, to be validated against
  reference accounts; it is never presented as measured.
- **Historical view** = weekly tickets by region, dated (completed) events only —
  stated limitation.

## Data

Registered corpus dependencies:
- `commercial/internal-upgrade-campaign`

Data we have: tickets, statuses, methods per device. Data we still need (stated gaps,
never faked): customer-side biomed time and rates (A-002 substitutes); ticket
severity/content (effort quality, not just count).

## Assumptions & expectations

- **A-002** — customer biomed cost model: active, low confidence, refresh on
  reference-account validation.
- Ticket counts are assumed attributable to the campaign rows they ride on; untagged
  support traffic is out of scope.

## Assertions & limits

This answer asserts: ticket rates on a consistent attempted basis, rollback sites, and
a clearly-assumed customer-cost range. It does **not** assert: ticket root cause,
customer satisfaction, or a measured customer cost. Demo data; not for clinical use.
