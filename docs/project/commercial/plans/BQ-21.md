## Question

What's open on the field-action docket, how timely are our MDRs, and what's the exposure if FDA walks in tomorrow?

**Asked by**: ceo, audit-committee · **cadence**: monthly

## Goal — the decision this answer serves

The monthly regulatory-exposure read for the CEO and audit committee. "If FDA walks in
tomorrow" = the concrete list an investigator would find: open field actions, MDRs open
against a running deadline, and any late filings in the recent record. The decision
served: whether regulatory-affairs posture needs intervention this month (resourcing,
escalation, disclosure to the audit committee) or is clean.

## Approach

Operational definitions this answer commits to:

- **Two deterministic anchors, both from the pin, no clocks.** Event-window
  calculations (the trailing on-time rate) anchor on the latest *event* date in the
  snapshot (max of opened/filed dates). Deadline-urgency calculations (days remaining
  on open items) anchor on the **snapshot date** (the edition's pin id) — urgency is
  about *now*, and the snapshot date is the closest deterministic proxy for now.
  Both anchors are stated in the report.
- **On-time** = `filed_date` ≤ `regulatory_deadline`. **Late** = filed after deadline
  (days late reported per item). **Trailing-12-month on-time rate** = share of MDRs
  whose `filed_date` falls inside the 365 days ending at the event anchor. Filing
  date, not opened date, places an MDR in the window: the rate measures filing
  behavior. Late filings *outside* the window still appear in the lifetime view —
  the window narrows the rate, it does not hide the record.
- **Basis sensitivity is published, not absorbed.** Alongside the committed basis,
  the report shows the on-time rate under the other defensible bases (pin-date-anchored
  filed-date window, opened-date window, lifetime), states whether the published basis
  is the most favorable of them, and states whether the E-21.1 verdict flips under any
  basis — all computed from the pin (added after an adversarial-verification caveat
  that the committed basis was the most favorable and the alternates were unshown).
- **Open item** = `status: open` (no filing yet for MDRs; field action not closed).
  Days-to-deadline is reported per open item against the snapshot-date anchor;
  negative values would mean an already-blown deadline and are flagged as such.
- **Docket roll-up** = counts by record type (mdr / correction / fsca / removal) ×
  status (open / filed / closed), so the "what's on the docket" answer is one table.
- **FSCA rollout state** is parsed from the record's description where it is stated
  regionally (NA / EMEA / APAC); it is reported qualitatively as recorded — this log
  tracks the regulatory report, not per-site completion telemetry.
- **The exposure list** ("if FDA walks in tomorrow") = every open item plus every
  late filing in the trailing window, enumerated with IDs — never just a count.

Data we have: the docket export (33 records, 2025-01 → 2026-07). Data we still need

decision log, not this docket.

## Assumptions & expectations

- **E-21.1** — Every MDR is filed within its regulatory deadline _(basis: 30-day MDR
  filing requirement (21 CFR 803) [VERIFY against docs/external distillation] —
  unvalidated, challengeable)_. Evaluated over the trailing-12-month window per the
  committed definition; lifetime late filings are reported alongside.
- The docket is assumed complete for reportable events — this answer audits filing
  timeliness of what was docketed, not whether everything reportable was docketed
  (see the stated decision-trail gap).
- The snapshot-date anchor assumes the export is current as of its acquisition date;
  an aged pin would understate urgency (pin freshness is separately linted).

## Assertions & limits

This answer asserts: the docket roll-up by type and status, the trailing-12-month MDR
on-time rate with each late filing named, days-to-deadline on every open item, and the
FSCA rollout state as recorded. It does **not** assert: completeness of the docket
(reportability decisions are out of scope), regulatory sufficiency of any individual
filing's content, per-site field-action completion (no telemetry dataset), or a legal
judgment of exposure — it equips the audit-committee conversation, it does not render
counsel's opinion.
