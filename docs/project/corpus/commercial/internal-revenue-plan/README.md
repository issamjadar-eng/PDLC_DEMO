# Corpus dataset — `commercial/internal-revenue-plan`

_Demo sample data — not for clinical use._

**Demo-fabricated** plan of record (120 rows): FY2026 quarterly rows (2026-Q1..Q4 ×
product line × region) plus FY2027..FY2030 annual rows (product line, region `ALL`).
Generated by the seeded `gen.py` (seed 42) — the stand-in for the FP&A
long-range-plan export (plan version `LRP-2026.1`, approved 2026-02-10). Every row
carries its `regulatory_dependency`: cleared | letter-to-file | pccp-enabled |
new-submission.

**Narrative knobs seeded** (honest list — the plan is a literal negotiated table, no
random noise):

- Plan totals: FY2026 $105M → FY2027 $130M → FY2028 $170M → FY2029 $245M →
  FY2030 $350M.
- FY2026 is ~$103M `cleared` + $2M cloud-suite `letter-to-file` — near-term plan does
  not depend on future regulatory events. (Actuals in `internal-financials` annualize
  ~$95–100M: the plan is a stretch.)
- The predictive-monitoring bet lives inside `cloud-suite`: FY2030 carries $210M of
  pccp-enabled ($140M) + new-submission ($70M) revenue — inside the locked $180–240M
  band — and from FY2028 onward the majority of planned cloud-suite revenue sits
  behind those FUTURE regulatory events (FY2028: $40M of $60M).
- PP3500 carries a growing letter-to-file slice (hardware revisions), $4M→$12M across
  FY2027–FY2030; legacy PP3000/IP5000 planned down to $4M/$2M by FY2030.

**Consumed by**: BQ-03 (plan vs actuals, joined with `internal-financials`), BQ-05
(regulatory-dependent revenue at risk), BQ-28 (recurring-revenue trajectory).

## Conventions

- Managed by the `corpus` skill (`corpus.py`). Snapshots under `snapshots/` are immutable —
  never hand-edit; re-acquire instead. `dataset.yml` is the config; `latest` points at the
  newest valid snapshot. Assumptions in `assumptions/A-NNN.yml`, waivers in `waivers/W-NNN.yml`.
- Regenerate via `corpus.py acquire` — never edit CSVs. Plan changes = a new plan
  version in `gen.py`'s literal tables, then re-acquire.
- `max_age_days: 365` — a plan of record turns over annually, not quarterly.
