## Approved Intended Use

### FDA-Cleared Intended Use Statement

> The MicroDose Pro (Model SP-6000) is intended for precise controlled delivery of medications and fluids via syringe in hospital settings, including critical care, neonatal, and pediatric units. The device is designed for use by qualified healthcare professionals for adult, pediatric, and neonatal patients requiring precise low-volume infusions.

### Additional Intended Use Details

**Patient Population**:
- Adults (≥18 years)
- Pediatric patients (all ages)
- Neonates (including premature infants ≥500g)

**Clinical Settings**:
- Intensive Care Units (ICU/SICU/MICU/CCU)
- Neonatal Intensive Care Units (NICU)
- Pediatric Intensive Care Units (PICU)
- Operating Rooms
- Emergency Departments
- Cardiac Catheterization Labs

**Administration Routes**:
- Intravenous (IV) - peripheral and central lines
- Intra-arterial
- Epidural/intrathecal (for specific medications)

## Clinical Data Summary

### Clinical Studies Supporting Clearance

#### Study 1: Flow Rate Accuracy Study
- **Study Design**: Bench testing and clinical validation
- **Test Conditions**: Multiple syringe sizes (5-60 mL), flow rates (0.01-500 mL/hr)
- **Primary Endpoint**: Flow rate accuracy at low rates (0.01-10 mL/hr)
- **Results**:
  - Flow rate accuracy: ±2.8% (within ±5% specification)
  - VTBI accuracy: ±0.005 mL at low volumes
  - Bolus accuracy: ±3.1%
  - Occlusion alarm response time: <30 seconds at all flow rates

#### Study 2: Multi-Center Clinical Performance Study
- **Study Design**: Prospective, multi-center study
- **Study Sites**: 8 US hospitals (4 adult ICU, 4 NICU/PICU)
- **Patient Enrollment**: 450 patients (200 adult, 150 pediatric, 100 neonatal)
- **Study Duration**: 12 months
- **Results**:
  - Device-related adverse events: 0.04%
  - User satisfaction: 93% (satisfied or very satisfied)
  - Programming time: 30% faster than predicate device
  - Syringe loading errors: 0.2% (all detected by device before delivery)
- **Publication**: Crit Care Med 2017;45(9):e892-e900

#### Study 3: Human Factors Validation
- **Study Design**: Formative and summative human factors testing
- **Participants**: 36 clinicians (RNs, RTs, MDs from ICU and NICU)
- **Study Tasks**: Device setup, syringe loading, programming, stacking, alarm response
- **Results**:
  - Task success rate: 98.9%
  - Critical use errors: 0
  - Close calls: 2 (both detected and corrected by device safeguards)
  - User satisfaction: 9.2/10 average

## Contraindications

### Absolute Contraindications

1. **Medication Factors**:
   - Medications requiring flow rates >500 mL/hr
   - Medications incompatible with polycarbonate syringes
   - Vesicant medications via peripheral IV (without appropriate monitoring)

2. **Clinical Conditions**:
   - Emergency resuscitation requiring rapid bolus infusion (use manual push)
   - Patients requiring MRI scanning (device is not MRI-safe)

3. **Device Factors**:
   - Use with non-compatible syringes (not on approved list)
   - Use with damaged or expired syringes
   - Use in explosive atmospheres or near flammable anesthetics

### Relative Contraindications (Use with Caution)

- Extremely low birth weight infants (<500g) - requires additional monitoring
- High-risk medications (chemotherapy, vasoactives) - requires drug library activation
- Home infusion - not approved for home use

## Warnings and Precautions

### Device-Specific Warnings

1. **Syringe Loading**:
   - Ensure syringe is correctly identified by device before starting infusion
   - Verify syringe size matches drug library selection
   - Inspect syringe for cracks, air bubbles, or particulate matter
   - Ensure syringe plunger moves freely without binding

2. **Programming Errors**:
   - Verify all programmed parameters before initiating therapy
   - Use independent double-check for high-risk medications
   - Pay special attention to decimal point entry (potential for 10x/100x dose errors at low rates)
   - Verify correct units (mL/hr vs mL/min)

3. **Free-Flow Prevention**:
   - Always ensure syringe is properly clamped by device before removing from pole
   - Never manually advance syringe plunger while in device
   - Verify syringe clamp is engaged during loading and removal
   - Use anti-reflux valves for central lines in neonates

4. **Occlusion Response**:
   - Respond immediately to occlusion alarms - increased pressure can cause syringe fracture
   - Do not increase occlusion limit above recommended setting for medication type
   - Inspect entire infusion line for kinks, clamps, or obstructions
   - For neonatal patients, use low occlusion settings (0.5-3 psi)

5. **Air Detection**:
   - Device detects air in syringe barrel but not in downstream tubing
   - Prime all tubing completely before connecting to patient
   - Use in-line filters for high-risk patients (neonates, central lines)

6. **Battery Management**:
   - Connect to AC power whenever available, especially for critical medications
   - Replace device if battery life is significantly reduced
   - Do not use device with damaged or swollen battery
   - For multi-pump stacks, verify all pumps have adequate battery charge

7. **Drug Library**:
   - Update drug library regularly per institutional formulary changes
   - Verify drug library includes neonatal/pediatric dosing limits
   - Document all hard limit overrides with physician order
   - Soft limit alerts should trigger clinical review before override

8. **Multi-Pump Stacking**:
   - Maximum 6 pumps in a stack
   - Verify stack stability before use - secure to pole or use stabilizing base
   - Master pump controls shared functions - verify master pump battery status
   - Individual pumps maintain independent delivery if master pump fails

### Precautions

- **Training**: All users must complete device training before clinical use; specialized training for NICU use
- **Maintenance**: Perform preventive maintenance per manufacturer schedule (every 12 months or 2,000 runtime hours)
- **Cleaning**: Use only approved cleaning agents; avoid alcohol >70% and bleach >0.5%
- **Syringe Compatibility**: Use only approved syringe brands listed in IFU
- **Environmental**: Protect device from moisture, extreme temperatures, and physical damage
- **Drop Protection**: Device may malfunction if dropped - inspect and test after any drop
- **Neonatal Use**: Extra caution required for neonates - use low occlusion limits and verify drug library neonatal mode

## Marketing and Promotional Considerations

### Approved Marketing Claims

**Can Claim** (with substantiation):
✅ "Precise delivery from 0.01 - 500 mL/hr"
✅ "Suitable for adult, pediatric, and neonatal patients"
✅ "Automatic syringe recognition for 5-60 mL syringes"
✅ "Comprehensive drug library with 400+ medications including neonatal"
✅ "Stackable up to 6 pumps for multi-medication therapy"
✅ "Wireless EMR integration and remote monitoring"
✅ "8+ hours battery life per pump"

**Cannot Claim** (not substantiated or false):
❌ "Eliminates medication errors" (too absolute)
❌ "Best syringe pump on the market" (superiority claim without head-to-head data)
❌ "FDA-approved" (it's FDA-cleared, not approved)
❌ "Zero programming errors" (false)
❌ "100% accurate" (has ±5% tolerance)
❌ "Prevents all free-flow events" (multiple layers but not absolute)
❌ Off-label uses not in cleared intended use (e.g., home use)

### Key Messaging Guardrails

1. **Must Align with Intended Use**: All marketing must reflect FDA-cleared intended use
2. **Cannot Promote Off-Label**: No promotion for home use or non-approved settings
3. **Must Include Balance**: Benefits AND limitations must be presented fairly
4. **Must Be Substantiated**: All claims must have supporting data
5. **Cannot Minimize Risks**: Programming errors, free-flow risk, and occlusion risks must be acknowledged
6. **Neonatal Use**: Extra emphasis on training and drug library for NICU applications
7. **Cannot Compare Without Data**: Competitive claims require head-to-head studies
