# FDA Guidance — The Abbreviated 510(k) Program

**Full Title**: The Abbreviated 510(k) Program — Guidance for Industry and Food and Drug Administration Staff
**Document Date**: September 13, 2019
**Status**: Final (Contains Nonbinding Recommendations)
**PDF Source**: FDA guidance database, document number 905; docket FDA-2019-D-4015
**Issuing Bodies**: CDRH, CBER
**Supersedes**: The Abbreviated 510(k) content of "The New 510(k) Paradigm — Alternate Approaches to Demonstrating Substantial Equivalence in Premarket Notifications" (March 20, 1998)
**Full text**: [`source-md/abbreviated-510k.md`](source-md/abbreviated-510k.md)

## Scope

This guidance describes an **optional 510(k) submission type** — the second of the two alternatives carved out of the 1998 New 510(k) Paradigm (the other is the Special 510(k); see [`special-510k-distilled.md`](special-510k-distilled.md)). An Abbreviated 510(k) demonstrates substantial equivalence by **relying on one or more of three anchors** — FDA guidance document(s), special controls for the device type, and/or voluntary consensus standard(s) — and presenting the supporting evidence as **summary reports** rather than complete test data. It does not alter the statutory SE criteria (FD&C §§ 510, 513; 21 CFR 807 subpart E) or the 21 CFR 807.87 content requirements.

**Key-question TL;DR**: An Abbreviated 510(k) fits when the substantive review questions for the device type are already answered by **established external benchmarks** — a device-specific guidance, codified special controls, or recognized consensus standards — so FDA can review *conformity summaries* instead of *raw evidence*. Unlike a Special 510(k), it is **not restricted to changes to the manufacturer's own device** (it can support a first clearance against a competitor predicate) and carries **no shortened review clock** — FDA reviews within the standard 90-day statutory deadline (FD&C § 510(n)(1)) and MDUFA goals; the efficiency gain is in preparation and review effort, not the deadline. If FDA decides the submission is not appropriate for the program, it **converts it to a Traditional 510(k)** and notifies the submitter.

## Section-by-Section Distillation

### I–II. Introduction & Background

- Voluntary program established 1998 under the New 510(k) Paradigm; this 2019 final restates it as a standalone guidance. Intent: conserve industry and Agency resources without altering the statutory SE criteria.
- Statutory roots of the three anchors: special controls from the Safe Medical Devices Act of 1990 (FD&C § 513(a)(1)(B) — performance standards, postmarket surveillance, patient registries, guidelines, etc., providing reasonable assurance of safety/effectiveness for Class II); voluntary consensus standards from FDAMA 1997 § 204 (FD&C § 514(c)).
- Companions for content/format: 21 CFR 807.87, *Refuse to Accept Policy for 510(k)s*, *Format for Traditional and Abbreviated 510(k)s*, and *Appropriate Use of Voluntary Consensus Standards in Premarket Submissions for Medical Devices*.

### III. The Program — The Three Reliance Anchors and the Summary-Report Mechanism

A manufacturer may choose an Abbreviated 510(k) when the submission relies on one or more of:

1. **FDA guidance document(s)** — typically a device-specific guidance that prescribes the testing expected for the device type;
2. **Special controls** — either in a device-specific classification regulation or a special controls guidance document; and/or
3. **Voluntary consensus standard(s)** — FDA-recognized standards per the Recognized Consensus Standards Database, or general use of a standard with supporting basis.

**Summary-report mechanics (guidance / special-controls reliance):** the submission includes a summary report describing how the guidance was used to demonstrate SE and/or how the device complies with the special control(s). The report summarizes the device description, the manufacturer's device design requirements, risk management information, and the test methods used to address performance characteristics. **Deviations must be disclosed**: any alternative methods used that are not described in the relied-upon guidance, with sufficient detail to justify the alternative approach.

**Declaration-of-conformity mechanics (consensus-standard reliance):** the submission provides a **declaration of conformity (DOC)** to the recognized standard, as recommended by *Appropriate Use of Voluntary Consensus Standards in Premarket Submissions for Medical Devices* (that guidance carries the DOC content expectations — standard edition, testing entity, deviations, ISO/IEC 17050-style supporting documentation). Two reliance modes:

- **DOC to an FDA-recognized standard** — the conformity declaration itself substitutes for the underlying data on the issues the standard covers.
- **General use of a standard** (non-recognized, or used outside its recognized scope) — the submission must include the **basis of such use plus the underlying information or data** supporting how the standard was used; general use does not earn the data-suppression benefit.

**Gap-filling duty:** issues *not* covered by any guidance, special control, or standard must still be addressed with data/information — the Abbreviated format only compresses what the anchors cover.

**Review posture:** standard acceptance review (RTA policy) and the standard 90-day clock / MDUFA goals — no expedited deadline. FDA's stated belief is that review *may* be more efficient because staff can rely on the summary reports. **Conversion**: if FDA does not believe the submission is appropriate for the program, it converts it to a Traditional 510(k) and notifies the submitter.

### Appendix A. Abbreviated 510(k) Content

- Coversheet clearly identifying the submission as an **"Abbreviated 510(k)"**;
- Full 21 CFR 807.87 content: device description, intended use / indications for use, proposed labeling (format per the RTA policy and *Format for Traditional and Abbreviated 510(k)s*);
- For guidance / special-controls reliance: the **summary report** (device description, design requirements, risk management information, test methods; justification for any alternative approaches);
- For consensus-standard reliance: DOC and supporting content per *Appropriate Use of Voluntary Consensus Standards*;
- **Data/information for issues not covered** by the anchors; and
- Indications for Use form (FDA 3881).

## When an Abbreviated 510(k) Is Worth Using

- **Strong fit**: mature device types with a device-specific FDA guidance or codified special controls that prescribe the full test battery, and/or device types whose performance questions are fully covered by recognized consensus standards (e.g., electrical safety / EMC / sterilization / biocompatibility batteries) — review reduces to conformity-checking summary reports.
- **Weak fit**: novel technological characteristics, performance questions with **no recognized standard or device-specific guidance** (e.g., device-specific clinical or algorithm performance), or SE arguments that turn on FDA's evaluation of underlying comparative data. Anything the anchors don't cover must carry full data anyway — if that residue is the heart of the SE argument, the Abbreviated format saves little and risks conversion to Traditional.
- **Process-standard caveat**: DOCs to *process* standards (software lifecycle, usability engineering, risk management, secure development) are routinely filed inside Traditional 510(k)s too. The Abbreviated type adds value only when the anchors carry the **substantive performance review**, not merely the process story.
- **No clock advantage**: unlike the Special 510(k) (30-day goal), the Abbreviated rides the standard cycle. Choose it for preparation/review efficiency and predictability, not speed.

## Practical Checklist (Manufacturer Self-Assessment)

1. Is there a device-specific FDA guidance, a special-controls regulation/guidance, or a set of FDA-recognized consensus standards that covers the **substantive** performance questions for this device type?
2. Can every covered question be presented as a summary report or DOC — with deviations disclosed and justified?
3. What remains uncovered? If the residue includes the core SE evidence (novel performance science, comparative clinical/algorithm data), the Abbreviated format adds little — file Traditional.
4. Standard-reliance leg: is each standard FDA-recognized in its current edition (check the Recognized Consensus Standards Database), and is use within the recognized scope? If not, prepare the general-use basis + underlying data.
5. Remember: standard 90-day clock, RTA applies, and FDA may convert to Traditional. When coverage is uncertain, ask in a Pre-Sub.
