## Overview

IEC 60601-1 is the umbrella "general standard" of the 60601 family. Per the publisher's abstract (<https://webstore.iec.ch/en/publication/67497>), it contains requirements for **basic safety** and **essential performance** that apply generally to medical electrical (ME) equipment; for particular equipment types those requirements are supplemented or modified by **collateral standards** (60601-1-x) and **particular standards** (60601-2-x). The Edition 3.2 consolidated version incorporates corrigenda 1 (2006), 2 (2007), and 3 (2022), the corrigendum to Amendment 1 (2014), and interpretation sheets 1 (2008), 2 (2009), 3 (2013) plus the interpretation sheet to Amendment 1 (2021).

Core concepts the standard is organized around (high-level, from the public abstract and the family's public structure):

- **Basic safety** — freedom from unacceptable risk directly caused by physical hazards (electrical, mechanical, thermal, radiation) under normal and single-fault conditions. `[VERIFY]`
- **Essential performance** — the clinical performance whose loss or degradation results in unacceptable risk; the manufacturer identifies it via the ISO 14971 risk management process. `[VERIFY]`
- **Single fault condition / single fault safe** — equipment must remain safe when any one means of protection fails. `[VERIFY]`
- **Means of protection (MOP)** — layered protection against electric shock, split into means of operator protection (MOOP) and means of patient protection (MOPP). `[VERIFY]`
- **Applied parts** — parts that contact the patient, classified Type B / BF / CF by degree of protection. `[VERIFY]`

## What the Audit Needs From This File

Consuming projects typically cite IEC 60601-1 from design inputs ("meets the general electrical-safety and essential-performance requirements of IEC 60601-1") and from hazard-analysis risk controls. When auditing such citations against this file, the recurring needs are:

1. **Edition designation.** Programs often cite "3rd edition + A1" (= Edition 3.1, IEC 60601-1:2005+A1:2012). The current FDA recognitions are **Edition 3.2** (19-49, IEC consolidated 2005+A1:2012+A2:2020, with US national differences) and the **AAMI ES60601-1** consolidated text including AMD2:2021 (19-46). An audit must check whether the DHF's cited edition matches the recognition the submission will declare conformity to — a "3rd edition + A1" citation is not automatically the FDA-recognized version and should be reconciled or flagged.
2. **Electric-shock topics** (leakage currents, insulation/dielectric, applied-part isolation, MOOP/MOPP) — recall places these under Clause 8 `[VERIFY]`; hazard registers commonly cite them as design controls for electrical-shock hazards. The evidence artifact is a third-party (accredited-lab) safety test report; this file can confirm the topic belongs to the standard, but pass/fail limits live only in the standard itself.
3. **Mechanical robustness / enclosure integrity** — recall places these under Clauses 9 and 15 `[VERIFY]`; hazard registers commonly cite them as controls for drop/impact/mount-failure hazards. Same evidence pattern: third-party safety test report against the applicable clauses.
4. **Alarm-function integrity** — the general standard covers the equipment; alarm-system behavior is governed by collateral IEC 60601-1-8 (FDA 5-131). Citations that pair "60601-1 essential performance" with "60601-1-8 alarm scheme" are the normal pattern for alarm-failure hazards.
5. **Family completeness** — a device citing the general standard almost always owes an explicit applicability determination for 60601-1-2 (EMC), 60601-1-6 (usability), 60601-1-8 (alarms), any environment collaterals (1-11 home, 1-12 EMS), and the equipment-type particular standard (for infusion devices, 60601-2-24 — whose FDA recognition status must be independently confirmed, see table above).

The project-specific applicability decisions (which device, which DIs/hazards cite it, which edition the program declares) live in the consuming project's `docs/external/standards/iec-60601-1.md` — not here.
