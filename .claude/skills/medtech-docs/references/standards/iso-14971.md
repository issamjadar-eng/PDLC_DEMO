# ISO 14971 — Application of Risk Management to Medical Devices

**Standard**: ISO 14971:2019 (3rd edition)
**Category**: Risk Management
**Recognized by FDA**: Yes — referenced in 510(k) SE guidance, SW Changes guidance, Cybersecurity guidance

## Overview

ISO 14971 specifies the process for manufacturers to identify hazards associated with medical devices, estimate and evaluate associated risks, control those risks, and monitor the effectiveness of controls. It applies across the entire device lifecycle — from design through post-production.

## Key Process Requirements by Clause

### Clause 4 — General Requirements for Risk Management

#### 4.1 Risk Management Process
- Establish, document, and maintain an ongoing risk management process
- The process must be applied throughout the entire lifecycle of the device
- **Deliverable**: Risk Management Plan

#### 4.2 Management Responsibilities
- Top management must ensure adequate resources for risk management
- Define policy for determining acceptable risk (risk acceptability criteria)
- Review risk management activities at planned intervals

#### 4.3 Competence of Personnel
- Personnel performing risk management must be competent based on education, training, skills, and experience
- Competence must be documented

#### 4.4 Risk Management Plan
- Plan must include at minimum:
  - Scope of the risk management activity (which device/device functions)
  - Assignment of responsibilities and authorities
  - Requirements for review of risk management activities
  - Criteria for risk acceptability (including when residual risk must be reduced)
  - Verification activities
  - Activities related to production and post-production information
- **Deliverable**: Risk Management Plan

#### 4.5 Risk Management File
- Results of risk management must be documented in a risk management file
- The file provides traceability for each identified hazard through the entire process
- **Deliverable**: Risk Management File (collection of records and documents)

### Clause 5 — Risk Analysis

#### 5.1 Risk Analysis Process
- Intended use and reasonably foreseeable misuse must be identified
- Identify characteristics related to safety (per Annex C)
- Identify hazards and hazardous situations
- Estimate the risk(s) for each hazardous situation

#### 5.2 Intended Use and Reasonably Foreseeable Misuse
- Document the intended use including the intended patient population
- Document the intended user profile
- Identify reasonably foreseeable misuse

#### 5.3 Identification of Characteristics Related to Safety
- Systematic identification of safety-related characteristics
- Consider characteristics in both normal and fault conditions
- Annex C provides a comprehensive list of questions to consider

#### 5.4 Identification of Hazards and Hazardous Situations
- Identify known and foreseeable hazards
- Consider hazards during all lifecycle phases (transport, storage, use, maintenance, disposal)
- For software: include data integrity errors, algorithm failures, incorrect outputs, timing failures, interface errors

#### 5.5 Risk Estimation
- For each identified hazardous situation, estimate the associated risk
- Risk = severity of harm x probability of occurrence of harm
- Severity categories must be defined in the risk management plan
- Probability categories must be defined in the risk management plan
- When systematic estimation is not possible, list hazardous situations for risk evaluation

### Clause 6 — Risk Evaluation

- For each identified risk, evaluate using the risk acceptability criteria from the risk management plan
- Decide whether risk reduction is needed
- Risk evaluation must be documented

### Clause 7 — Risk Control

#### 7.1 Risk Control Option Analysis
- When risk reduction is required, identify risk control measures
- Use the following priority order:
  1. **Inherent safety by design** — eliminate or reduce the hazard
  2. **Protective measures** in the device itself or manufacturing process
  3. **Information for safety** — labeling, instructions, training
- Document the rationale for selecting risk control measures

#### 7.2 Implementation of Risk Control Measures
- Implement the selected risk control measures
- Verify implementation
- Document the implementation

#### 7.3 Residual Risk Evaluation
- After risk control measures are implemented, evaluate the residual risk
- If residual risk is not acceptable, apply additional risk control measures
- Perform benefit-risk analysis when residual risk cannot be further reduced

#### 7.4 Benefit-Risk Analysis
- When residual risk exceeds acceptability criteria, evaluate whether the medical benefit outweighs the residual risk
- If benefit does not outweigh the risk, the risk remains unacceptable
- Benefit-risk analysis must be documented

#### 7.5 Risks Arising from Risk Control Measures
- Evaluate whether the risk control measures introduce new hazards or hazardous situations
- If new hazards are introduced, apply the full risk management process to them

#### 7.6 Completeness of Risk Control
- Verify that all identified hazardous situations have been considered
- Ensure the residual risk for each hazardous situation is acceptable

### Clause 8 — Evaluation of Overall Residual Risk

- After all risk control measures are applied, evaluate the overall residual risk
- Consider the cumulative effect of all residual risks
- If overall residual risk is unacceptable, apply additional risk control measures
- Document the overall residual risk evaluation

### Clause 9 — Risk Management Review

- Before commercial release, review the risk management process
- Verify the risk management plan has been executed
- Verify the overall residual risk is acceptable
- Verify appropriate methods are in place to collect post-production information
- Document review results in the risk management file
- **Deliverable**: Risk Management Report

### Clause 10 — Production and Post-Production Activities

- Establish a system to collect and review production and post-production information
- Information includes: user/patient feedback, complaints, adverse events, publicly available info about similar devices
- Evaluate information for relevance to safety
- If previously unrecognized hazards or risks are identified, feed back into the risk management process
