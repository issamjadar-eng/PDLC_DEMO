## Overview

ISO 10993 is a multi-part series governing the biological evaluation (biocompatibility) of medical devices — the process of judging whether a device's materials and their contact with the body pose an unacceptable biological risk. **Part 1 is the umbrella**: it frames biological evaluation as an activity *within* the ISO 14971 risk-management process, driving a device-specific evaluation plan from the device's material composition and its nature + duration of body contact. The **numbered test-method parts (-5, -10, -23, and others)** supply the standardized methods for the individual biological endpoints that Part 1's evaluation identifies as relevant.

A key discipline of the current framework: testing is **not** a fixed checklist. Part 1 directs the manufacturer to first assemble existing information (material characterization, chemical characterization, prior data, literature) and to conduct new endpoint tests only where a data gap remains for a relevant endpoint. FDA's guidance on the standard reinforces this risk-based, gap-driven posture.

## The 2021 -10 / -23 split (irritation moved to a new part) — KEY EDITION-DEPENDENT FACT

Before 2021, **ISO 10993-10** was titled *Tests for irritation and skin sensitization* and covered **both** endpoints. The 2021 revision **split** the two endpoints across two documents:

| Endpoint | Pre-2021 home | 2021+ home |
|----------|---------------|------------|
| Skin sensitization | ISO 10993-10:2010 | **ISO 10993-10:2021** (retitled *Tests for skin sensitization* — sensitization only) |
| Irritation | ISO 10993-10:2010 | **ISO 10993-23:2021** (new standalone part — *Tests for irritation*) |

Practical consequence for any document that attributes endpoints to a part: **"ISO 10993-10 = sensitization + irritation" is only correct for the :2010 edition.** Under the 2021 editions, an irritation obligation must cite **ISO 10993-23**, and -10 carries sensitization alone. Wherever a hazard analysis, design input, or test plan pins "-10 (sensitization/irritation)", the edition must be pinned too — the attribution is edition-dependent. `[VERIFY]` the exact FDA recognition status of -10:2021 and -23:2021 (both are recognized with **partial** scope — certain clauses/annexes are excluded as conflicting with FDA guidance) against the FDA database.

_Public sources for the split:_ ISO webstore / ANSI abstracts for [ISO 10993-23:2021](https://webstore.ansi.org/standards/iso/iso10993232021) and [ISO 10993-10:2021](https://www.iso.org/standard/75279.html); standards-industry write-ups (e.g., [NSF](https://www.nsf.org/knowledge-library/iso-10993-23-the-step-wise-approach-to-evaluate-potential-irritants-in-medical-devices-becomes-effective), [Congenius](https://congenius.ch/biological-evaluation-medical-devices-tests-for-irritation/)).

## The evaluation framework — nature × duration of body contact

ISO 10993-1 (and FDA's guidance on it) organize biological evaluation around **two axes that together select which biological endpoints are relevant** for a given device:

1. **Nature of body contact (contact category).** FDA's guidance describes evaluation by category of body contact — broadly **surface-contacting devices**, **external communicating devices**, and **implant devices**, each with sub-categories by the tissue contacted (e.g., intact skin; mucosal membrane; breached/compromised surface; blood path, indirect; tissue/bone/dentin; circulating blood) `[VERIFY exact category/sub-category wording against the guidance]`.
2. **Duration of contact.** Contact duration bands — commonly **limited** (short-term), **prolonged**, and **long-term / permanent** exposure `[VERIFY exact thresholds — the widely-cited bands are limited ≤24 h, prolonged >24 h–30 d, permanent >30 d, but confirm against the source]`.

The intersection of (category × duration) points to a set of **biological endpoints to be addressed** — for example cytotoxicity, sensitization, irritation (or intracutaneous reactivity), acute/subacute/subchronic/chronic systemic toxicity, material-mediated pyrogenicity, genotoxicity, implantation effects, and hemocompatibility (blood-contacting), among others. The specific endpoint set per cell of the matrix is defined in the standard/guidance and is **not reproduced here** — consult the source; treat any endpoint-per-cell recall as `[VERIFY]`.

> FDA's guidance **"Use of International Standard ISO 10993-1, 'Biological Evaluation of Medical Devices - Part 1: Evaluation and Testing Within a Risk Management Process'"** (issued 2020-09-04, superseding the 2016 guidance; updated 2023-09-08) is the public document that lays out FDA's version of this endpoint framework and its attachment matrices. It is the citable public anchor for the framework described above.
> — FDA landing page: <https://www.fda.gov/regulatory-information/search-fda-guidance-documents/use-international-standard-iso-10993-1-biological-evaluation-medical-devices-part-1-evaluation-and> · Federal Register availability notices [2016](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2016/06/16/2016-14190/use-of-international-standard-iso-10993-1-biological-evaluation-of-medical-devices-part-1-evaluation) / [2023](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2023/09/08/2023-19402/use-of-international-standard-iso-10993-1-biological-evaluation-of-medical-devices-part-1-evaluation). The lineage traces to FDA Blue Book Memorandum **#G95-1** (1995), which the 2016/2020/2023 guidances superseded.

The three endpoints most often triaged for a **limited-contact, intact-skin / indirect-fluid-path** device are **cytotoxicity (-5)**, **sensitization (-10)**, and **irritation (-23 since 2021; -10 pre-2021)** — the classic "big three" screen. Whether more endpoints are required is a device-specific determination driven by Part 1's risk-based evaluation, not a fixed rule `[VERIFY]`.

## For Claude (when grounded against a consuming project)

1. **Edition-pin every endpoint attribution.** If a project doc says "irritation per ISO 10993-10", check the edition: correct for :2010, **wrong** for :2021 (irritation is -23 since 2021, -10 is sensitization-only). Flag the mismatch; recommend pinning the edition.
2. **Part 1 is a process, not a test.** Biocompatibility "per ISO 10993-1" means a risk-based evaluation plan, not a fixed test battery. The specific tests (-5/-10/-23/…) are selected from the (contact category × duration) endpoint matrix.
3. **Cite the source, not this file, for clause/table content.** This is a finding aid — no verbatim clause or matrix content is reproduced. For the endpoint-per-cell matrix, FDA recognition scope, or exact thresholds, direct the reader to the standard or the FDA guidance.
