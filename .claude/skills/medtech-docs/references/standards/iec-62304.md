**Category**: Software & Development
**Recognized by FDA**: Yes — FDA-recognized consensus standard

## Overview

IEC 62304 defines the life cycle requirements for the development and maintenance of medical device software. It establishes processes for software development planning, requirements analysis, architectural design, detailed design, unit implementation, integration testing, system testing, release, and maintenance.

Amendment 1 (2015) introduced a refined safety classification scheme and clarified that software items (not just the full software system) can be individually classified.

## Clause 4 — General Requirements

### 4.1 Quality Management System
The manufacturer developing medical device software must do so within a quality management system (e.g., ISO 13485 or an equivalent quality system).

### 4.2 Risk Management
The manufacturer must apply a risk management process complying with **ISO 14971**. IEC 62304's Clause 7 software risk process operates inside — not instead of — the ISO 14971 process.

### 4.3 Software Safety Classification (as amended by A1:2015)

The manufacturer assigns a safety class (A, B, or C) to each **software system** according to the possible effects on the patient, operator, or other people resulting from a hazard to which the software system can contribute. The A1:2015 classification logic:

- **Class A** — § 4.3(a): the software system either **cannot contribute to a hazardous situation**, OR it can contribute to a hazardous situation that **does not result in unacceptable risk after consideration of risk control measures external to the software system**.
- **Class B** — the software system can contribute to a hazardous situation resulting in unacceptable risk after consideration of external risk control measures, and the resulting possible harm is **non-serious injury**.
- **Class C** — same as B, but the resulting possible harm is **death or serious injury**.

Key mechanics of § 4.3 (sub-clause lettering beyond (a) `[VERIFY against the amended standard]`):

- **Probability of software failure is assumed to be 1 (100%).** Classification is driven by *severity of the resulting harm* (after external risk controls), never by an estimated probability that the software will fail.
- **External risk controls can lower the class.** Risk control measures **external to the software system** (hardware interlocks, independent safeguards, other software systems, clinician review steps) may be considered when determining whether a hazardous situation results in unacceptable risk — this is the § 4.3(a) mechanism that allows a software system to be Class A even though it can contribute to a hazardous situation. The effectiveness (and independence) of those external controls is an **ISO 14971-linked expectation** — the credited controls must be real, evaluated risk-control measures living in the risk management file, not merely asserted — which flows from the ISO 14971 interlock and the general obligation to check risk controls for implementation/effectiveness, rather than necessarily from verbatim § 4.3 text `[substantiation locus VERIFY against the standard]`.
- **Default is Class C.** If no safety class has been assigned/documented for a software system (or software item), Class C requirements apply until a class is assigned.
- **Decomposition / item-level classification (A1:2015).** A software system may be decomposed into software items, and a software item may be assigned a lower class than its parent **only if** the items are **segregated** and the segregation rationale (why a failure of the lower-class item cannot affect the higher-class item) is documented. Absent documented segregation, an item inherits its parent system's class.
- The assigned class and its rationale are documented in the **risk management file**.

### 4.4 Legacy Software (added by A1:2015)
Defines a path for software developed before the standard's application: assess against post-market data, perform gap analysis against the standard's required deliverables, and document a rationale for continued use.

## Safety Classification Summary

| Class | A1:2015 criterion | Possible harm | Process burden |
|-------|-------------------|---------------|----------------|
| **A** | Cannot contribute to a hazardous situation, OR contribution does not result in unacceptable risk after external risk controls (§ 4.3(a)) | n/a | Lightest |
| **B** | Contributes to hazardous situation with unacceptable risk after external controls | Non-serious injury | Moderate |
| **C** | Contributes to hazardous situation with unacceptable risk after external controls | Death or serious injury | Full |

> Note: the pre-amendment 2006 scheme classified on severity alone. The A1:2015 scheme above — pivoting on *unacceptable risk after external risk controls* — is the operative one; classification arguments citing external risk controls rest on § 4.3(a).

> **Corroboration (external-source cross-check).** The two-limb Class A structure and the "risk control measures external to the software system" pivot are reproduced near-verbatim across multiple independent standards-consultancy and notified-body-adjacent references. Two points worth carrying: (1) the class-lowering control is **any** measure *external to the software system* — hardware, an **independent software system**, or an **operational / clinician (user-verification) control** — not hardware alone (the test is the software-system boundary, not a hardware test); (2) the external-control consideration lives in **§ 4.3 itself**, distinct from the § 4.3/§ 5.3 *segregation* mechanism (which keeps mixed-class items independent *within* one system) — so a class-reduction credit is cited to **§ 4.3(a)**. Watch-out: some vendor glossaries still present the stale *2006 severity-only* Class A ("no injury possible"); that is an un-updated simplification, not a competing reading. This cross-check raises confidence but does **not** replace the standard — for filing-load-bearing wording (the § 4.3 note text and any external-control effectiveness/independence obligation) confirm against a licensed copy of IEC 62304:2006/AMD1:2015 § 4.3.

## Key Process Requirements by Clause

### Clause 5 — Software Development Process

#### 5.1 Software Development Planning
- Establish a software development plan before development begins
- Plan must cover: life cycle model, deliverables, traceability, configuration management, risk management integration
- Plan must define software development standards, methods, and tools
- Plan must address verification and validation strategies
- **Deliverable**: Software Development Plan (SDP)

#### 5.2 Software Requirements Analysis
- Define and document software requirements from system requirements and risk control measures
- Requirements must address: functional/capability requirements, inputs/outputs, interfaces, alarms/warnings, security, usability, data definition, installation/deployment, operation/maintenance
- Requirements must be verifiable
- Re-evaluate risk analysis when requirements are established or changed
- **Deliverable**: Software Requirements Specification (SRS)
- **Class applicability**: Required for A, B, C

#### 5.3 Software Architectural Design
- Define and document the software architecture
- Architecture must transform requirements into a design that describes the structure and identifies software items
- Architecture must describe interfaces between software items and external components
- Identify SOUP (Software of Unknown Provenance) items
- Evaluate architecture for support of risk control measures
- **Deliverable**: Software Architecture Document (SAD)
- **Class applicability**: Required for B, C (not required for A)

#### 5.4 Software Detailed Design
- Subdivide software into software units (5.4.1)
- Develop and document a detailed design for each software unit, sufficient to permit implementation and verification
- Verify detailed design implements the architecture
- **Class applicability**: 5.4.1 (subdivision into units) required for **B and C**; documented detailed design per unit and its verification (5.4.2 onward) required for **C only** `[VERIFY sub-clause split against Table A.1]`

#### 5.5 Software Unit Implementation
- Implement each software unit per the detailed design
- Establish unit verification criteria
- **Class applicability**: Required for A, B, C (verification level varies)

#### 5.6 Software Integration and Integration Testing
- Integrate software units according to the integration plan
- Verify that integrated software items meet the software requirements
- Test interfaces between software items and with hardware
- Document integration test results
- **Class applicability**: Required for B, C

#### 5.7 Software System Testing
- Test the integrated software system against software requirements
- Test must cover: normal conditions, foreseeable misuse, stress/boundary conditions
- Document system test results with pass/fail criteria
- **Deliverable**: Software System Test Report
- **Class applicability**: Required for **B, C** (not required for Class A)

#### 5.8 Software Release
- Ensure all activities are complete before release
- Document known anomalies and their evaluation
- Verify configuration items are identified and version-controlled
- **Deliverable**: Software Release Package
- **Class applicability**: Required for A, B, C

### Clause 6 — Software Maintenance Process

- Establish a maintenance plan
- Monitor feedback from users and production, including problem reports
- Analyze and implement modifications using the same process as initial development

### Clause 7 — Software Risk Management Process

- Integrate software risk management into the software development process
- Identify hazardous situations caused by software, including SOUP
- Evaluate risk control measures implemented in software
- Verify risk control implementation
- Manage risks from SOUP items
- **Ties to**: ISO 14971 — IEC 62304 does not replace 14971 but extends it for software

### Clause 8 — Software Configuration Management

- Identify configuration items (software items, documentation, tools)
- Control changes to configuration items
- Maintain traceability between configuration items

### Clause 9 — Software Problem Resolution Process

- Prepare a problem resolution process
- Investigate and evaluate problems
- Advise relevant parties of problems
- Track problem resolution to closure
- Analyze trends in problems

## SOUP (Software of Unknown Provenance)

IEC 62304 has specific requirements for SOUP (third-party/open-source software):
- Identify all SOUP items used
- Document SOUP item name, manufacturer, version
- Evaluate SOUP anomaly lists for relevance
- Define functional and performance requirements for SOUP items
- Risk-evaluate SOUP items per Clause 7

## FDA Mapping Notes

- FDA does NOT directly map IEC 62304 safety classes to Basic/Enhanced documentation levels.
- **"Level of Concern" (LoC) is obsolete.** FDA's June 2023 guidance *Content of Premarket Submissions for Device Software Functions* (which replaced the 2005 guidance) eliminated the Minor/Moderate/Major LoC scheme. The current **Documentation Level (Basic vs Enhanced)** is determined by the guidance's enumerated factors — Enhanced applies when the device (1) is a combination-product constituent part, (2) is intended for use in blood/blood-component testing or manufacture, (3) is Class III, or (4) a failure or flaw of any device software function could present a probable risk of death or serious injury (to patient, user, or others in the use environment). Otherwise Basic. See `../fda-guidance/sw-functions-distilled.md`.
- An IEC 62304 class is not an input to that determination, but a Class C conclusion (death/serious injury possible) and Enhanced factor (4) examine closely related questions; document both analyses consistently.
- The Device Software Functions guidance accepts a Declaration of Conformity to IEC 62304 in lieu of some documentation elements — conformance strengthens a 510(k) submission.
