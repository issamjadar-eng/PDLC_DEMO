## Overview

IEC 60601-2-24:2012 specifies particular basic-safety and essential-performance requirements for infusion pumps and volumetric infusion controllers. Per the publisher's abstract and the public preview's scope section, it covers enteral nutrition pumps, infusion pumps for ambulatory use, syringe or container pumps, volumetric infusion controllers, and volumetric infusion pumps — and it applies to administration sets insofar as their characteristics influence the pump's basic safety or essential performance (it does not otherwise specify requirements for administration sets). Excluded: devices intended for diagnostic or similar use (e.g., angiography pumps under continuous operator control), extracorporeal blood circulation, implantable devices, urodynamics and male-impotence diagnostic equipment, and devices covered by ISO 28620.

Per the foreword (public preview, p. 4), the second edition is a technical revision aligned to IEC 60601-1:2005+A1:2012 **with new clause numbering, including usability and alarms** — so citations carried over from 1st-edition (1998) documents will not match the 2012 numbering.

## The 201.x Numbering Convention

Verified from the public preview (subclause 201.1.4, "Particular standards", pp. 8–9):

- Clauses/subclauses of this particular standard carry the prefix **201** and correspond to the same-numbered clause of the general standard — e.g., **201.12 modifies Clause 12 of IEC 60601-1** ("Accuracy of controls and instruments and protection against hazardous outputs").
- Prefix **20x** addresses collateral standard x — e.g., **202** modifies IEC 60601-1-2 (EMC), **206** modifies IEC 60601-1-6 (usability), **208** modifies IEC 60601-1-8 (alarms). Per 201.1.3, IEC 60601-1-3 does not apply.
- Subclauses, figures, and tables **additional** to the general standard are numbered **from 201.101 upward** (e.g., 201.12.1.102); additional definitions from **201.3.201**; additional annexes lettered AA, BB, etc.
- Modifications are flagged as *Replacement* / *Addition* / *Amendment* relative to the general or collateral standard text.

Practical consequence: a citation like "§201.12.1.103" designates an *added* subclause under the particular standard's modification of general-standard Clause 12.1 — its topic cannot be inferred from IEC 60601-1's own 12.1; it must be read in IEC 60601-2-24 itself.

## Key Topic Areas

### Flow accuracy and the trumpet curve

The standard's accuracy tests (201.12.1.102–.107 per Table 201.101) characterize delivery accuracy over defined test periods, with results presented graphically. The public preview's figure list shows the apparatus and presentation forms: test apparatus for volumetric and syringe/container pumps (Figures 201.104a/b), start-up graphs over the first 2 h and over the stabilization period (Figures 201.105, 201.108, 201.110), and **trumpet curves** plotted from second-hour data, end-of-stabilization data, and the last hour of the administration-set change interval (Figures 201.106, 201.107, 201.109, 201.111). The trumpet curve plots worst-case (maximum and minimum) percentage deviation from the set rate over sliding observation windows of increasing duration — deviations are large over short windows and converge toward the overall mean error as the window lengthens, giving the characteristic trumpet shape (statistical background in Annex AA, Figures AA.101–AA.108). [VERIFY exact test durations, sample intervals, observation-window set, and back-pressure conditions against a licensed copy.]

Test rates are set per defined terms: the *intermediate rate* (201.3.208) is 25 ml/h for volumetric pumps/controllers and 5 ml/h for syringe or container pumps (manufacturer-typical for ambulatory pumps); *minimum rate* (201.3.211) is the lowest operator-selectable rate but not less than 1 ml/h; Table 201.102 fixes set rates, bolus volumes, and apparatus per pump type.

### Occlusion, unintended bolus, and maximum infusion pressure

Added definitions (public preview, 201.3): *occlusion alarm threshold* (201.3.214 — the value of the physical quantity at which the occlusion alarm activates), *unintended bolus* (201.3.221 — the unintended discrete quantity delivered after release of an occlusion), *intended bolus* (201.3.203), and *maximum infusion pressure* (201.3.210 — the maximum pressure the pump can generate under total patient-line occlusion). Table 201.101 assigns *protection against unintended bolus volumes and occlusion* to 201.12.4.4.104, and Figure 201.112 defines the test apparatus for determining occlusion alarm threshold and bolus volumes. Quantitative limits, alarm-time requirements, and disclosure obligations (e.g., time-to-occlusion-alarm at minimum rate) are in the non-public body — [VERIFY against licensed copy].

### Free flow

*Free flow* is defined at 201.3.205 — unintended flow to the patient through the administration set not controlled by the pump (e.g., gravity/pressure effects when the set is removed from the pump). The protective requirement against free flow sits in the 201.12.4.4.x protective-systems family — exact subclause number and requirement wording **[VERIFY against licensed copy]**.

### Alarms

Clause 208 modifies IEC 60601-1-8:2006 for infusion pumps: Table 208.101 assigns alarm-condition priorities to related situations, and Table 208.102 specifies characteristics of the pulse of auditory alarm signals; high-priority alarm signals per Table 208.101 are an essential-performance item (208.6.1.2.101 per Table 201.101). ## Relationship to Other Standards

- **IEC 60601-1:2005+A1:2012** — the general standard this particular standard modifies; a particular-standard requirement takes priority over the general standard.
- **IEC 60601-1-2:2007 (EMC), IEC 60601-1-6:2010 (usability), IEC 60601-1-8:2006 (alarms)** — collaterals applied as modified by clauses 202/206/208 (per 201.1.3 in the public preview). IEC 60601-1-3 does not apply.
- **ISO 8536-4** (infusion sets, gravity feed), **ISO 7864** (sterile hypodermic needles), **ISO 3696** (water for analytical use) — normative references added at 201.2.
- **ISO 28620** — non-electrically-driven portable infusion devices; explicitly out of scope.
