## Overview

IEC 60601-1-2 is the EMC collateral standard of the IEC 60601 series. Per the publisher's abstract and the FDA recognition record's scope statement, it applies to the **basic safety and essential performance** of medical electrical (ME) equipment and ME systems (a) **in the presence of electromagnetic disturbances** (immunity) and (b) with regard to **electromagnetic disturbances emitted by** the ME equipment/system (emissions). Basic safety with regard to electromagnetic disturbances applies to all ME equipment and ME systems.

The 4th edition restructured EMC testing around the device's **intended-use environment** rather than a one-size-fits-all test profile: per the publisher's abstract, the edition aligns immunity test levels with intended-use environments (professional healthcare facility, home healthcare, and special environments), adds requirements for immunity to **proximity fields from portable RF communications equipment**, specifies **port-specific** immunity tests, ties EMC into **risk management** concepts, and requires identification of immunity **pass/fail criteria**. Amendment 1 (2020, consolidated as Edition 4.1) refines these requirements. [VERIFY: the precise clause-level deltas between Ed 4.0 and Ed 4.1 against the amendment text]

As a collateral standard it hangs off IEC 60601-1 (General requirements, Clause 17 of the general standard points to it [VERIFY]) and is applied together with any particular (Part 2-x) standard for the device type, which may modify EMC requirements [VERIFY].

## Intended-Use Environment Categories

The standard scales immunity test levels to where the device is intended to operate. The three environment categories (named in the publisher's abstract):

| Environment | Characterization (paraphrase) | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Professional healthcare facility** | Hospitals, clinics — locations where patients are treated under continuous professional supervision [VERIFY: exact definition wording] | FDA's EMC guidance carries its own definition of "professional healthcare facility" that the agency treats as controlling (see FDA recognition carve-out #1 below) |
| **Home healthcare** | Dwellings and other places where patients live or transit, outside professional facilities; less controlled mains and RF conditions, generally higher immunity test levels [VERIFY: exact levels] | FDA does **not** recognize Figure 3's "nursing homes" example as part of the home healthcare environment (recognition carve-out) |
| **Special environments** | Locations with unique/intense disturbance sources (e.g., near active HF surgical equipment or inside RF-shielded imaging rooms [VERIFY: examples against the source]) — addressed through risk management and adjusted test levels [VERIFY] | The publisher's abstract confirms the edition "introduces guidance on special environments" |

Manufacturer's choice of environment category drives the immunity test plan and must match the intended use / use environment claimed in labeling and the risk management file [VERIFY: clause reference].

## Emissions

- Emissions requirements reference the **CISPR** basic emission standards; for most ME equipment the controlling document is **CISPR 11** (industrial, scientific and medical RF equipment) [VERIFY: which CISPR documents each port/equipment type maps to].
- [VERIFY — model recall, confirm against CISPR 11/the standard] CISPR 11 classifies equipment as **Group 1/Group 2** (whether RF energy is intentionally generated/used) and **Class A** (suitable for non-domestic environments) vs **Class B** (suitable for domestic establishments and buildings on the public low-voltage mains — the stricter limit set). A Class B emissions claim is the more conservative claim and remains valid in professional healthcare facilities.

## Immunity

- Immunity requirements are organized **per port** (enclosure port, input a.c. power port, signal input/output parts, etc.) with test levels set by environment category. Subclause **8.9 / Table 8** covers **conducted disturbances induced by RF fields** per IEC 61000-4-6 (subclause and table identification confirmed by the FDA recognition record, which carves out Table 8's Note k).
- [VERIFY — model recall, confirm against the source] The immunity phenomena draw on the IEC 61000-4-x basic standards: electrostatic discharge (61000-4-2), radiated RF fields (61000-4-3), electrical fast transients/bursts (61000-4-4), surges (61000-4-5), conducted RF (61000-4-6), power-frequency magnetic fields (61000-4-8), and voltage dips/interruptions (61000-4-11), plus the 4th edition's proximity-field test for portable RF communications equipment (spot-frequency tests representing common wireless services).

## Essential Performance During Immunity Testing

The load-bearing concept of the 4th edition: immunity testing is judged against **maintenance of basic safety and essential performance**, not merely "the device survived."

- The manufacturer identifies the device's **essential performance** (per IEC 60601-1) and derives **immunity pass/fail criteria** from it — the publisher's abstract confirms the edition requires "identification of immunity pass/fail criteria."
- During each immunity exposure, the device must maintain basic safety and essential performance, or degrade only in ways shown acceptable through **risk management** [VERIFY: exact allowance wording].
- This couples the EMC test report to the ISO 14971 risk file: the criteria used on the bench must trace to the essential-performance definition and the risk analysis, and a reviewer will check that linkage.

## FDA Recognition — Extent and Carve-Outs

Per the FDA Supplementary Information Sheet (Rec# 19-36, record 41539 — URL in header), recognition is **partial**; the following parts are **not** recognized:

1. **Figure 3, "nursing homes" as an example of the home healthcare environment** — conflicts with the definition of "professional healthcare facility" in FDA's final guidance *Electromagnetic Compatibility (EMC) of Medical Devices* (issued July 6, 2022).
2. **Subclause 8.9, Table 8, Note k** (under "Conducted disturbances induced by RF fields") — the note's exclusion of SIP/SOP cables shorter than 1 m from the IEC 61000-4-6 test is not recognized; FDA cites published literature supporting testing down to 0.4 m.

The recognition record lists as relevant FDA guidance/publications: *Electromagnetic Compatibility (EMC) of Medical Devices* (July 6, 2022), *Radio Frequency Wireless Technology in Medical Devices* (Aug 13, 2013), *Design Considerations for Devices Intended for Home Use* (Nov 24, 2014), AAMI CR500:2019, and IEC 61000-4-6 Ed 4.0.

## What Audits Need

What a reviewer or auditor checking an EMC claim against this standard looks for:

1. **The exact edition designation.** The FDA-recognized version is **IEC 60601-1-2 Edition 4.1 2020-09 CONSOLIDATED VERSION** (Rec# 19-36) — and as of the database query backing this file (May 2026 database revision), it is the **only** currently recognized version of Part 1-2; a declaration of conformity citing bare "4th edition (2014)" should be reconciled against the recognized 4.1 designation. Colloquially "4th edition" is often used for both; the DoC and test report must carry the precise designation.
2. **The environment category claim.** A predicate/subject device claiming **professional healthcare facility environments** must show immunity test levels selected for that category, and the claim must match the labeled use environment. (This is the pattern for a PCA-style infusion pump predicate citing professional-environment EMC — the environment claim in the EMC report, the IFU, and the risk file must agree.)
3. **Essential-performance-anchored pass/fail criteria** in the test report, traceable to the IEC 60601-1 essential-performance definition and the ISO 14971 risk file.
4. **Emissions class claimed vs demonstrated** (e.g., CISPR 11 Class B [VERIFY: classification framework per CISPR 11]) with test data within limits.
5. **The FDA carve-outs respected** — no reliance on Figure 3's nursing-home example for environment categorization, and SIP/SOP cables < 1 m not excluded from conducted-RF immunity testing on the strength of Table 8 Note k.
6. **Third-party test laboratory pedigree** — accredited lab; ASCA-accredited labs are relevant since the standard is in the ASCA program.
7. **Pairing with FDA's EMC guidance** (*Electromagnetic Compatibility (EMC) of Medical Devices*, July 6, 2022) — the recognition record itself points submissions to it; the guidance defines what EMC information FDA expects in a premarket submission [VERIFY: guidance content — not distilled in this registry].
