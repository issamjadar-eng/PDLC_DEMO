## Overview

IEC 60812:2018 establishes how failure modes and effects analysis (FMEA) — including the failure modes, effects and criticality analysis (FMECA) variant — is planned, performed, documented, and maintained. The purpose is to establish how items or processes might fail to perform their function so that required treatments can be identified. An FMEA provides a systematic method for identifying failure modes together with their effects on the item or process, both locally and globally; it may also identify the causes of failure modes. Failure modes can be prioritized to support treatment decisions. When the criticality ranking involves at least the severity of consequences (and often other importance measures), the analysis is known as FMECA.

The standard is applicable to hardware, software, processes (including human action), and their interfaces, in any combination. It is a **generic** standard: an FMEA performed under it can be used in a safety analysis for regulatory and other purposes, but the standard itself does not give safety-application-specific guidance.

## Method Elements

The FMEA/FMECA method as framed by the standard proceeds through these elements (element list distilled from the public scope/abstract; clause numbering not reproduced here — consult the standard for the normative clause structure):

- **Planning and scope definition** — define the item or process under analysis, its boundaries, functions, and interfaces, and tailor the analysis to the application context.
- **Failure mode identification** — for each function or element, systematically identify the ways it can fail to perform its function.
- **Effects analysis** — determine the consequences of each failure mode locally and at higher levels of the system (through to the end effect).
- **Cause identification** — optionally identify the causes underlying each failure mode.
- **Criticality / prioritization (FMECA)** — rank failure modes to support treatment decisions, using at least severity of consequences and typically additional importance measures. The 2018 edition describes **alternative means of calculating risk priority numbers (RPN)** and **criticality matrix** methods.
- **Reporting and maintenance** — document the analysis in one of several reporting formats (including database systems) and maintain it over the life of the item or process.

## What Changed in the 2018 (3rd) Edition

Per the IEC's published description of the edition, the 2018 revision:

- Provides generic normative requirements applicable across all industries, with informative annexes giving application examples (safety, automotive, software, service processes).
- Adds guidance on **tailoring** the analysis for specific contexts.
- Describes multiple reporting formats, including database systems.
- Introduces **alternative means of calculating RPN** and **criticality matrix** methods for ranking.
- Describes relationships to other dependability analysis methodologies.

[VERIFY] The 2018 edition is publicly characterized as broadening prioritization beyond the classic S×O×D RPN — the edition's published content list includes RPN alternatives and criticality matrices, and the standard is widely reported to document the limitations of arithmetic RPN products (ordinal-scale multiplication, non-unique rankings) as motivation for those alternatives. The specific clause/annex where the RPN-limitations discussion lives is recall-only and must be confirmed against the standard itself.

## Relationship to Medical Device Risk Management (ISO 14971)

In a medical-device program, FMEA under IEC 60812 is a **risk analysis tool used inside** the ISO 14971 risk management process — it is not a substitute for that process. ISO/TR 24971:2020 (guidance on applying ISO 14971) lists FMEA — as defined in IEC 60812 — among the techniques that support risk analysis (Annex B `[VERIFY — annex letter confirmed via secondary sources only]`), alongside PHA, FTA, ETA, HAZOP, and HACCP, and notes that no single technique by itself addresses all elements of a risk analysis required by ISO 14971. Practical consequences commonly drawn from this positioning:

- FMEA is bottom-up (single-fault, component/function-level) and complements top-down hazard analysis; it does not by itself produce the hazard–hazardous-situation–harm chain ISO 14971 requires.
- FMEA prioritization measures (RPN, criticality) support treatment decisions within the analysis; **risk acceptability** under ISO 14971 is judged against the manufacturer's severity × probability-of-harm criteria defined in the risk management plan, not against an RPN threshold.
