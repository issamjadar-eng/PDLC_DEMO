```yaml
verbatim: |
  "The manufacturer shall document a risk management plan for the device.
  The plan shall be part of the risk management file."
extracted_requirements:
  - "Produce a Risk Management Plan before risk management activities begin"
  - "Plan must be part of the Risk Management File — not a standalone document"
  - "Plan must be maintained and updated throughout the device lifecycle"
```

**Context:** The Risk Management Plan is the governing document for all risk management activities. It must exist before hazard analysis begins. For a multi-DHF project, one integrated plan at the system DHF level is the norm; item-level additions are permitted but must roll up.

```yaml
verbatim: |
  "The risk management plan shall include at minimum: (a) the scope of the risk
  management activities; (b) assignment of responsibilities and authorities;
  (c) requirements for review of risk management activities; (d) criteria for
  risk acceptability, including the criteria used to decide when further risk
  reduction is required; (e) method for evaluating overall residual risk;
  (f) verification activities; (g) activities related to collection and review
  of production and post-production information."
extracted_requirements:
  - "Define the scope — which device, which functions, which lifecycle phases"
  - "Assign responsibility and authority for risk management activities (named roles)"
  - "Define risk acceptability criteria — severity and probability scales, risk matrix, acceptable thresholds"
  - "Specify the method for evaluating overall residual risk (§8)"
  - "Identify planned verification activities linking risk controls to their evidence"
  - "Describe how post-production information will be collected and fed back into the risk file"
```

**Context:** §4.4(d) is FDA's most frequently cited gap — risk acceptability criteria must be quantified or at least operationally defined before any risk evaluations can be performed. A vague "risks shall be acceptable" statement is non-conforming.


**Context:** The risk matrix and acceptance criteria must be established in the Plan (before analysis) and applied consistently in the File (during analysis). Changing criteria after evaluating risks is a common audit finding.

## §4.5 — Risk Management File

<a id="OBL-14971-004"></a>

```yaml
id: OBL-14971-004
title: "Risk Management File"
source: "ISO 14971:2019 §4.5"
section: "§4.5 — Risk management file"
scope_flags: []
topic: risk-management
artifact_type: record
dhf_owner: system
min_iec62304_class: A
canonical_role: risk-management
criticality: must-have
applies_to:
  - role: risk-management
    file_pattern: "*risk-management-file*.md"
verbatim: |
  "The manufacturer shall maintain a risk management file. For each hazard
  identified, the risk management file shall allow traceability to: the hazard,
  the hazardous situation(s), the estimated risk(s), the risk control measures,
  and the residual risk(s) after implementation of the controls."
extracted_requirements:
  - "Maintain a Risk Management File — a collection of records providing full traceability for each hazard"
  - "Each hazard must be traceable through: hazardous situation → estimated risk → risk control → residual risk"
  - "File must be structured to support audit — an auditor must be able to follow one hazard end-to-end"
```

**Context:** The Risk Management File is not a single document — it is the collection of all risk management records (plan, hazard analysis, FMEA, risk control evidence, ORRE, report). The traceability requirement is the normative basis for a hazard-to-control trace matrix.

<a id="OBL-14971-007"></a>

```yaml

title: "Hazard Identification"
source: "ISO 14971:2019 §5.4"
section: "§5.4 — Hazard identification"
scope_flags: []
topic: risk-management
artifact_type: record
dhf_owner: both
min_iec62304_class: A
canonical_role: risk-management
criticality: must-have
applies_to:
  - role: risk-management
    file_pattern: "*preliminary-hazard-analysis*.md"
  - role: risk-management
    file_pattern: "*hazard-analysis*.md"
  - role: risk-management
    file_pattern: "*risk-management-file*.md"
verbatim: |
  "The manufacturer shall identify hazards and hazardous situations. For each
  identified hazardous situation, the manufacturer shall estimate the associated
  risk using available information or data."
extracted_requirements:
  - "Identify all foreseeable hazards (energy, biology, data, software, mechanical, chemical, etc.)"
  - "For each hazard, trace the sequence of events to a hazardous situation (hazard → failure mode → hazardous situation → harm)"
  - "Consider hazards during all lifecycle phases: normal use, foreseeable misuse, maintenance, storage"
  - "Document results in a Preliminary Hazard Analysis or equivalent hazard table in the Risk Management File"
```

**Context:** For SaMD, hazards commonly include: incorrect algorithm output, data integrity failure, UI presentation error, connectivity failure causing loss of guidance, cybersecurity breach causing data corruption. Each hazardous situation must be traced to a harm — intermediate steps in the causal chain must be documented.

<a id="OBL-14971-008"></a>

```yaml
id: OBL-14971-008
title: "Risk Estimation"
source: "ISO 14971:2019 §5.5"
section: "§5.5 — Risk estimation"
scope_flags: []
topic: risk-management
artifact_type: record
dhf_owner: both
min_iec62304_class: A
canonical_role: risk-management
criticality: must-have
applies_to:
  - role: risk-management
    file_pattern: "*risk-assessment-fmea*.md"
  - role: risk-management
    file_pattern: "*risk-management-file*.md"
verbatim: |
  "For each identified hazardous situation, the manufacturer shall estimate the
  associated risk. The estimation shall consider the probability of occurrence of
  harm and the severity of that harm."
extracted_requirements:
  - "Estimate risk for each hazardous situation using severity × probability from the risk matrix in the plan"
  - "Assign qualitative or quantitative values using the scales defined in the Risk Management Plan"
  - "Where probability cannot be estimated, list hazardous situations for risk evaluation without numerical estimation"
  - "Document risk estimations in the risk management file (e.g., FMEA table)"
```

**Context:** Risk estimation must use the severity and probability scales established in the Risk Management Plan — they cannot be applied ad-hoc. For AI-enabled functions, probability of harm is often estimated using algorithm performance data (e.g., false-negative rate for a critical classification).

## §6 — Risk Evaluation

<a id="OBL-14971-009"></a>

```yaml
id: OBL-14971-009
title: "Risk Evaluation"
source: "ISO 14971:2019 §6"
section: "§6 — Risk evaluation"
scope_flags: []
topic: risk-management
artifact_type: record
dhf_owner: both
min_iec62304_class: A
canonical_role: risk-management
criticality: must-have
applies_to:
  - role: risk-management
    file_pattern: "*risk-assessment-fmea*.md"
  - role: risk-management
    file_pattern: "*risk-management-file*.md"
verbatim: |
  "The manufacturer shall evaluate each identified risk using the risk acceptability
  criteria in the risk management plan. This evaluation determines whether risk
  reduction is required."
extracted_requirements:
  - "Apply the risk matrix from the plan to each estimated risk — classify as acceptable / ALARP / unacceptable"
  - "Document the evaluation decision: accept / reduce further / apply benefit-risk analysis"
  - "If reduction is required, proceed to §7 risk control — do not accept an unacceptable risk without justification"
```

**Context:** Risk evaluation is a binary gate: either the risk is acceptable as-is, or it requires reduction. Unacceptable risks that proceed without documented rationale are a critical audit failure. For Class C SaMD, FDA reviewers will scrutinize any unacceptable risk accepted without a §7.4 benefit-risk analysis.

## §7 — Risk Control

<a id="OBL-14971-010"></a>

```yaml
id: OBL-14971-010
title: "Risk Control Options"
source: "ISO 14971:2019 §7.1"
section: "§7.1 — Risk control option analysis"
scope_flags: []
topic: risk-management
artifact_type: record
dhf_owner: both
min_iec62304_class: A
canonical_role: risk-management
criticality: must-have
applies_to:
  - role: risk-management
    file_pattern: "*risk-control-measures*.md"
  - role: risk-management
    file_pattern: "*risk-management-file*.md"
verbatim: |
  "The manufacturer shall identify risk control measures, using one or more of the
  following, in priority order: (a) inherent safety by design; (b) protective
  measures in the device itself or manufacturing process; (c) information for
  safety."
extracted_requirements:
  - "Apply risk controls in priority order: (1) design-level elimination, (2) device protective measure, (3) information/labeling"
  - "Document the rationale for selecting the chosen control type — explain why higher-priority options were not applicable when skipped"
  - "For software: design-level controls include defensive coding, input validation, output limits, redundancy; labeling-only for a software-induced hazard is rarely sufficient at Class C"
```

**Context:** The priority order is mandatory — you cannot jump to information-for-safety (labeling, IFU) if a design fix is feasible. For AI outputs, this means algorithmic confidence thresholds or output clamping (design) should be exhausted before relying on user warnings alone.

```yaml
  "The manufacturer shall implement the risk control measures and verify their
  implementation. After implementation, the manufacturer shall evaluate the
  residual risk."
extracted_requirements:
  - "Verify that each risk control measure is implemented as specified (implementation verification record)"
  - "Evaluate residual risk after controls — re-estimate severity × probability with controls in place"
  - "If residual risk remains unacceptable, apply additional controls or proceed to §7.4 benefit-risk analysis"
  - "Link each risk control to its V&V evidence in the Risk Management File"
```

**Context:** Implementation verification is distinct from V&V testing — it confirms the control exists in the design, while V&V testing confirms it works. Both are needed. The link between risk controls and V&V protocols is the most critical trace in the DHF for FDA reviewers.

**Context:** Benefit-risk analysis is not a catch-all escape — it must be specific to the hazardous situation, not a generic device-level argument. FDA reviewers expect to see at least one formal benefit-risk analysis for Class II SaMD covering the highest-severity residual risks.


**Context:** Secondary hazard evaluation is commonly skipped. A classic example: adding an alert for a safety condition may create a new hazard (alert fatigue, nuisance alarms). For software, adding input validation may create denial-of-service risk; adding logging may create privacy risk.

## §8 — Overall Residual Risk Evaluation

<a id="OBL-14971-014"></a>

```yaml

title: "Overall Residual Risk Evaluation"
source: "ISO 14971:2019 §8"
section: "§8 — Evaluation of overall residual risk"
scope_flags: []
topic: risk-management
artifact_type: record
dhf_owner: system
min_iec62304_class: A
canonical_role: risk-management
criticality: must-have
applies_to:
  - role: risk-management
    file_pattern: "*overall-residual-risk-evaluation*.md"
  - role: risk-management
    file_pattern: "*risk-management-report*.md"
verbatim: |
  "After all risk control measures have been implemented, the manufacturer shall
  evaluate the overall residual risk of the device using the criteria established
  in the risk management plan. If the overall residual risk is not acceptable,
  the manufacturer shall gather and review information to determine whether the
  medical benefit outweighs the overall residual risk."
extracted_requirements:
  - "Conduct an Overall Residual Risk Evaluation (ORRE) after all individual risk controls are verified"
  - "ORRE considers the cumulative effect of all residual risks — not just individual hazardous situation risks"
  - "Document the ORRE conclusion: overall residual risk acceptable / requires benefit-risk analysis"
  - "If overall benefit-risk analysis is required, document it formally (not embedded in the ORRE)"
```

**Context:** The ORRE is a pre-release gate. It must be completed before the §9 Risk Management Review. For a multi-module device, the ORRE must consider the combined risk picture across all modules (Pre-Op + Intra-Op + Mgmt Services interactions).

## §10 — Production and Post-Production Activities

<a id="OBL-14971-016"></a>

```yaml
id: OBL-14971-016
title: "Post-Production Risk Feedback"
source: "ISO 14971:2019 §10"
section: "§10 — Production and post-production activities"
scope_flags:
  - pccp
topic: post-market
artifact_type: plan
dhf_owner: system
min_iec62304_class: A
canonical_role: postmarket
criticality: must-have
applies_to:
  - role: postmarket
    file_pattern: "*post-market-surveillance-plan*.md"
  - role: risk-management
    file_pattern: "*risk-management-plan*.md"
verbatim: |
  "The manufacturer shall establish a system to collect and review information
  generated during production and post-production phases. If previously unrecognized
  hazards or risks that are no longer acceptable are identified, the manufacturer
  shall feed this information back into the risk management process."
extracted_requirements:
  - "Establish a Post-Market Surveillance (PMS) system that collects: complaints, adverse event reports, field feedback, publicly available information about similar devices"
  - "Review PMS data on a defined cadence against the risk management file"
  - "If new hazards or newly unacceptable risks are identified, formally feed back into the risk management process"
  - "For PCCP: post-market data from each change iteration must feed back into risk evaluation before the next change is authorized"
```

**Context:** This is the normative basis for the PMS Plan's link to risk management. The feedback loop is mandatory — post-market data that reveals new hazards must update the risk file, not just the complaint log. For a PCCP product, this loop runs continuously: each deployed change generates field data that informs the next change's risk evaluation.
